**A-3① Channel I**（位置：L136 `\section{Channel I: Formal Fiction into Physics}` 后、L138 `\subsection{The historical chain}` 前；净增约 6 打印行）

```latex
Why does the typology begin here? The formal channel is the limiting case in which the fiction is purest --- a system with no referent, no scenario, no claims about anything --- and therefore the hardest case for the idea that fiction can do scientific work. If a language with no intended subject matter can be shown to have carried physics, the loop closes in its barest form; if it cannot, the idea retreats to the softer forms and must survive there. ```latex
```
```latex
Channel II takes the opposite end of the form spectrum from Channel I. A scenario is not a language waiting for a theory: it is bounded, internally run, and epistemically contested --- the one form of fiction whose working is debated by philosophy in the open, case by case. That openness is why the channel matters here: where the mechanism of a fiction's work is itself a live question, the checks cannot assume an answer, and the paper's refusal to adjudicate the dispute is a constraint the channel imposes rather than a choice the paper made.
```

**A-3③ Channel III**（位置：L198 `\section` 行后、L200 `\subsection{Entropy's two journeys}` 前；净增约 7 打印行）

```latex
Between the purity of Channel I and the openness of Channel II, the quantitative channel contributes the form that changes the terms of the question: a fiction that is a number. An ensemble with no material location is not a language and not a scenario; once a quantity is defined over it, the fiction acquires the properties numbers have --- it can be computed, priced, and compared --- and the channel's history can be written in those terms, with a formula where the other channels have a text or a thought. The gain is precision; the cost is the one downgrade in the typology, recorded where it falls in \S4.
```

```latex
The narrative channel is the form most readily dismissed as mere influence, and its inclusion is methodological rather than sentimental: narrative is the one form of fiction whose entry into science leaves a datable trail of reception --- inquiries, letters, published answers --- rather than a diffusion of mood. The difficulty here is not dating the trail but crediting it, and the channel is included precisely so that the three checks, rather than reputation, decide how much an agenda-setting story can be credited with.
```
