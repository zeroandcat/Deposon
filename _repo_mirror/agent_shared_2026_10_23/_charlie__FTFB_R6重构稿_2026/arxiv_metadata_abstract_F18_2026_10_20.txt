When may fiction count as an input to the exact sciences, rather than as their contrast case? We argue that fiction is not evidence but a recurrent, checkable input: four channels — formal, controlled, quantitative, narrative — separate under three checks (temporal priority, dispensability, operative residue), and whether machine worldviews open the reverse channel stands or falls on the same falsifiability criteria we specify. Each channel is stated against its strongest available objection and answered on the record, concessions included. The four channels are each anchored in cases: non-Euclidean geometry as formal language prior to physical reference; thought experiments as controlled fiction under a translatability criterion; entropy as an abstract quantity that entered engineering by formal analogy and re-entered physics carrying a measurable price; and narrative fiction that supplies vocabulary and sets agendas, from a wormhole question to the singularity. A mapping (M1–M5) pairs the hardness criteria of hard science fiction with the falsifiability criteria of AI research claims in a constructed structural parallel — criteria with shared failure modes, one row an import from the pre-registration literature, declared as such — and a four-stage pipeline — setting, formalization, pre-registration, experiment — ports the mapping into a worked method, demonstrated on one engineering-grade, mid-strength artifact at the stated power, with its negative results disclosed in full. The reverse channel is not asserted; an adjudication agenda states what would count as feedback and how it would die. Every claim is stated to be checkable by a third party; the frozen artifacts and full digests are deposited in a public repository, cited at its frozen commit, and the instance's temporal anchor is an author-attested freeze, recorded as provisional (§7.4).
