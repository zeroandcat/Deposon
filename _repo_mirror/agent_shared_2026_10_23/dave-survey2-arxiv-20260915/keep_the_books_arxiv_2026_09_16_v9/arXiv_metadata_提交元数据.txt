Abstract (从 v9 tex 提取的纯文本，243 词 / 1844 字符，纯 ASCII，arXiv 表单合规):
When a large language model emits a chain of reasoning, what claim can a third party recompute? The axis is keep the books: a claim is on-axis when its support is a frozen record an auditor with stated resources can mechanically recheck. Five research lines map onto the axis: process verification (auditing steps); chain-of-thought faithfulness (the explanation is not the computation); evaluation integrity (the benchmark's own books); game-theoretic verification (incentives make honesty checkable); and conservation and physical priors (invariants as guarantors). Six contributions. (i) Operational definitions: auditability as third-party recomputability, falsifiability as executability of pre-registered verdicts, bookkeeping as the commitment-execution-verification loop. (ii) A three-axis taxonomy (organization, attribute, evidence strength) with mutual-exclusion and coverage arguments. (iii) A five-line survey reporting failure modes and negative results as first-class content, spanning the reasoning-model era. (iv) The assembled matrix and cross-line lessons: pre-registered kills as trust infrastructure, verification-cost economics, the recursion bottom of audit stacks, and no systematized escrow verification for capability claims. (v) A case study instantiating all six attributes across the five lines, under full parity and the same three-tier discipline, with three falsified claims and a value proposition relocated by pre-registered controls. (vi) The audit outlook: machine worldviews and AI scientists as the next claimants needing books; machine-generated knowledge without recomputable books is the closing problem. Its own bookkeeping follows its subject: the novelty search is completed, the positioning claim retained, and no reference appears beyond the 60-entry verified base plus the disclosed self-citation.
