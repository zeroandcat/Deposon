# Fiction That Feeds Back: From Non-Euclidean Geometry to Machine Worldviews

**Abstract.** Whether fiction has ever contributed to the exact sciences is usually treated as a matter of anecdote. This paper treats it as a decidable question. Fiction is not evidence, but it is a recurrent, decidable input to the exact sciences: four channels — formal, controlled, quantitative, narrative — separate under three checks (temporal priority, dispensability of the fictional input, operative residue), and whether machine worldviews open the reverse channel stands or falls on the same falsifiability criteria we specify. We score the four channels against a closed pool of thirty-nine verified references: formal fiction supplied physics with self-consistent language prior to reference; controlled fiction is routine production material whose yield survives translation into checkable argument; a fictional quantity can enter engineering by isomorphism and return to physics at a measurable cost; narrative fiction both supplies vocabulary and sets agendas. A method section maps the hard-SF criteria onto the falsifiability criteria of AI research claims through a four-stage pipeline — setting, specification, pre-registration, experiment — and one engineering-grade instance is walked through all four stages, scored on the same table as the historical cases, with its negative results, including three killed propositions, disclosed in full. The reverse channel, from machine worldviews back into science, is not asserted: we state the three conditions under which it becomes decidable, and what finding would kill it.


## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 From decoration to input

Fiction, in the standard account of the exact sciences, is what the exact sciences are not: it decorates the work, motivates it, and supplies the contrast against which rigor is defined. This paper takes a narrower and more mechanical view of such encounters. We ask when a piece of fiction — a formal system built with no intended referent, a thought experiment, a quantity defined over an abstract ensemble, a story — can be shown to have entered the exact sciences as a working input; and, symmetrically, we ask what it would take for such an entry to fail. Treated this way, the subject is no longer a matter of taste or anecdote. It admits dated anchors, explicit criteria, and verdicts that a third party can re-derive.

Our subject is a two-way loop between fiction and the exact sciences. The forward direction runs from fiction into science, and we argue that it divides into four channel types — formal, controlled, quantitative, narrative — each documented by anchored cases and each scored on the same three checks (§2–§5). The reverse direction — machine worldviews feeding back into the sciences — is not asserted anywhere in this paper. It is set up as an open question with named criteria (§8), because whether it exists is exactly the kind of claim that can die under a pre-registered adjudication. The business of this paper is criteria, cases, and scores, not celebration. A methodological section (§6) supplies the mapping that makes the criteria portable between the two literatures; a case section (§7) exhibits them running end to end on an engineering artifact; and a closing section (§9) turns the criteria on the paper itself.

### 1.2 The claim, its falsifiability conditions, and the three checks

The thesis of this paper is:

*Fiction is not evidence, but it is a recurrent, decidable input to the exact sciences: four channels — formal, controlled, quantitative, narrative — separate under three checks (temporal priority, dispensability of the fictional input, operative residue), and whether machine worldviews open the reverse channel stands or falls on the same falsifiability criteria we specify.*

Three delimitations are part of the claim, not qualifications bolted onto it. We do not claim that fiction is necessary to science; we do not claim that science descends from fiction; and we do not claim that the reverse channel exists. The word "decidable" carries its ordinary methodological sense: the contribution credited to a fictional input must be confirmable or refutable by named checks, not by taste.

The claim is falsifiable in four specified ways.

- **F-C1 (typology).** If the four channel types prove pairwise inseparable under the three checks — if the checks return the same profile for types that are supposed to differ — the four-way typology is weakened.
- **F-C2 (channel).** If the flagship case of any channel is shown to have a dispensable fictional input, or to fail the temporal check, that channel is downgraded.
- **F-C3 (reverse channel).** If hypotheses generated by AI systems, run under pre-registered adjudication, systematically leave no kill line to hang — no falsifiable consequence that a decisive test can reject — then the proposed reverse channel ("machine worldviews feed back into science") is declared dead.
- **F-C4 (non-collage).** If new cases cannot be classified without ambiguity by the same criteria, the typology is ad hoc.

The three checks are applied to every channel, section by section, and consolidated in §9.1.

- **T (temporal priority).** The fictional input must precede the scientific use it is credited with, and the precedence must be anchorable — by etymology and dictionary record, by primary text, or by participant self-statement.
- **D (dispensability).** An account of the same scientific development that omits the fictional input must be visibly less parsimonious. If an equally parsimonious fiction-free account exists, the channel's contribution is downgraded from necessity to available convenience.
- **R (operative residue).** The encounter must leave an operative residue — a vocabulary, a quantity, an agenda item, or a formal system that continues to do work after the encounter.

Two evidence tiers appear in this paper and are never mixed. Historical evidence is graded by anchor type — primary-source anchor, self-statement anchor, lexical anchor, quantitative study — and no historical claim is worded to need more than its tier supports. The engineering case of §7 is graded on the pre-registered adjudication tier, a different instrument for a different kind of product. Sections §2–§5 use the historical tier alone.

### 1.3 Contributions and scope

The paper makes four contributions.

1. **A criterion-based typology of fiction-to-science feedback**: four channel types, each with its core claim, its anchored cases, and a score on the same three checks (§2–§5).
2. **A structural mapping between the "hardness" criteria of hard science fiction and the falsifiability criteria of AI research claims**, with a four-stage pipeline that ports the former into the latter (§6).
3. **One engineering-grade, mid-strength instance of the full pipeline**, on record and examined in §7 on the same footing as the historical cases, scored by the same criteria, with its negative results disclosed in full.
4. **An adjudication agenda for the reverse channel**: what would count as machine worldviews feeding back into science, stated so that it can fail (§8).

Four scope declarations bound what follows. (i) This is a position paper with an intellectual-historical core; its historical sections are case studies, not a systematic history. (ii) The reference pool is closed: thirty-nine items, cited by pool number [R01]–[R39]; every entry was verified against primary records, and two entries carry unstable identifier snapshots, flagged in the reference list and to be completed before submission. (iii) The historical evidence tier and the pre-registered adjudication tier are distinct instruments (§1.2); nothing in the historical sections borrows the authority of the latter. (iv) Uniqueness-flavored statements — including the statement that no integrated treatment of the four channels exists — are made strictly relative to our three-line search completed on 2026-09-16; a second-round novelty check is scheduled before submission, and those statements carry that gate.

A note on numbers: every number in this paper is either bibliographic (a year, a volume, a page range from the pool) or a formula named in the text. The term "machine worldview" appears here as it appears in the thesis sentence; its definitional anchor, and the debate it sits in, are cited in §8.

## 2. Channel I: Formal Fiction into Physics

### 2.1 The historical chain

A geometry can be built before there is anything for it to describe. The nineteenth century produced consistent alternatives to Euclidean geometry as pure formal constructions, and for a long period these structures referred to nothing physical: they were languages in search of a subject matter. We use "formal fiction" in exactly this sense — a self-consistent symbolic system with no intended physical referent at the time of its construction — and not as a rhetorical flourish. The term is chosen to make the channel's input state checkable: for any candidate case, whether the system was built without the later physical use in view is a historical question with a datable answer.

The scholarly record of how one such language was taken up by physics is documented in detail by Walter [R01]. Minkowski's four-dimensional reformulation of special relativity carried a recognizably non-Euclidean style, and — the point on which this channel turns — the style was itself part of what was transmitted: not the mathematics alone, but a way of writing, teaching, and receiving relativity that made the non-Euclidean vocabulary integral to how the theory was communicated to the physics community. Walter's study treats that style as a historiographical object in its own right, traceable through exposition and reception rather than deducible from the mathematics it carries. The chain continues through general relativity, whose formulation took Riemannian geometry as its working language. By then the direction of travel was fixed: a formal apparatus originally developed with no physical referent had become the standard medium in which a physical theory was stated. The geometry did not need the physics in order to be consistent; the physics arrived later and found the language ready.

That order — the language precedes the empirical joining — is the signature of this channel, and unlike most signatures in intellectual history it is anchorable in primary texts: the constructions, the reformulation, and the participant's account of the join all carry dates. Channel I is presented here before the others because its evidence is the most document-like of the four; the reader should treat it as the template for what an anchored channel looks like, not as the strongest claim the paper makes.

### 2.2 The join, in the participant's testimony

Einstein's 1921 lecture "Geometry and Experience", published the following year [R02], is the participant testimony for how the join was made, and its structure matters as much as its content. Einstein's account separates pure geometry — a formal system whose certainty holds precisely insofar as it abstains from reference to reality — from applied geometry, which acquires empirical content through a stipulated coordination between formal terms and physical situations. Certainty and reference are, on this account, a trade: the moment geometry says anything about the world, its truth is no longer the geometrician's alone. In general relativity the trade is carried by the field laws together with the geometry: the coordination is an act performed inside physics, not a property waiting inside the fiction to be discovered.

For the channel this does two kinds of work. It fixes the temporal order — the formal system is available before the coordination — and it fixes the epistemic division of labor: the fictional input supplies structure, while the physical commitment is added later, by a documented decision of the person best placed to know. Here the join has a participant account, which is as direct as historical anchoring gets. The trade also explains why the channel's residue is a language rather than a fact: what survives into physics is a way of stating theories, and the empirical content lives in the coordination, not in the geometry.

Wigner's "unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics in the natural sciences" [R03] names the residual surprise of the pattern: formal structures built for internal reasons so often turn out apt for physics. We use it as the channel's counter-question, not as its explanation. The name records that something is unexplained after the anchors are laid; it does not tell us what the fictional input did, and we do not ask it to. The channel claim below is graded so as not to spend more than the anchors support — which is precisely why the claim is about language supply rather than about the aptness Wigner found remarkable.

### 2.3 Channel claim and assessment

**Channel claim.** Formal fiction supplies self-consistent language prior to reference, and the feedback occurs as language preceding the empirical joining.

**Channel assessment.**

- *T (temporal priority).* The formal structures precede their physical deployment: the constructions predate the uses, the transmission of the non-Euclidean style into physics is documented in the scholarly record [R01], and the join is fixed by the participant's own account [R02]. Every link is primary-text anchorable. **Pass.**
- *D (dispensability).* Here the channel is deliberately graded down, and the scholarship says so: Walter's study shows the non-Euclidean style was not the unique path into relativity — it was one presentation among available alternatives, adopted for reasons that include notation and pedagogy as much as necessity [R01]. A history of the same physics without the style is not visibly less parsimonious. The check therefore downgrades the claim from necessity to supply: the fictional language was available, adopted, and consequential — not indispensable. This is the check doing its job: it is what prevents the channel claim from drifting into a necessity thesis. **Partial pass; the claim is stated at the supply level.**
- *R (operative residue).* The residue is a formal-geometric language that remains the standard medium of the theory — an operative residue of exactly the kind that the temporal anchor and the supply-level downgrade leave in place. **Pass.**

The asymmetry in the scores is informative rather than embarrassing. Walter's non-uniqueness evidence [R01] sets the internal tier of the channel: the language supply is strongly documented — the style existed, was available, and was adopted — while the necessity of the style is not claimed, because the same physics had available presentations that did without it. "Language prior to reference" is exactly the claim this tier supports, and the wording above does not exceed it.

## 3. Channel II: Controlled Fiction

### 3.1 The thought-experiment dispute

Thought experiments are the canonical case of fiction doing scientific work, and the philosophical literature contains a live dispute over how the work is done. On the realist side, Brown [R04] holds that in the stock cases rehearsed by this literature, thought experiments yield new knowledge of laws of nature in a way that is not reducible to derivation: the scenario, once constructed, shows something that running through premises does not by itself deliver. On the eliminativist side, Norton [R05] holds that a thought experiment is an argument in picturesque clothing: its epistemic work is fully done by a reconstructible argument from premises to conclusion, and the picturesque imagery is a vehicle for presenting the argument, not an independent source of warrant. The dispute is live, the two positions are incompatible as accounts of mechanism, and this paper does not adjudicate them. The case is canonical for this paper for a structural reason: the fictional status of the input is not in dispute — the scenario describes an experiment that was not run — so the dispute isolates exactly the question the channel faces, what does the fiction contribute and how would we know, with none of the fog that surrounds weaker senses of "fiction."

What matters for the channel is a point the two sides share. Whatever the yield of a thought experiment is, both parties hold that it can be exhibited in a checkable form — Brown by isolating the insight and identifying what it is insight into, Norton by reconstructing the argument that does the work. The disagreement is over where the epistemic weight sits, not over whether the result can be laid out for inspection. Translatability into a checkable form is therefore the common ground of the dispute, and it is exactly the property the channel criterion needs: the channel can be run on either side's terms, because either side can produce the checkable artifact. The stakes of the dispute for this paper are narrow, and that narrowness is deliberate — the channel needs a checkable artifact, and the dispute's structure guarantees one under either resolution.

### 3.2 Model-based reasoning and fictionalism

Nersessian [R06] places the thought experiment inside a wider class of model-based reasoning: scientific concepts are created through the iterative construction of imagistic, analogical, and extreme-case models, and the fictional scenario is one carrier of this modeling practice. Suárez's collection [R07] supplies the fictionalist frame for the same territory: idealizations and fictions can represent without describing anything real, and the representational success of a fiction does not presuppose a corresponding real property. Both lines move the question from "is the fiction true?" to "what work does the fiction do, and how is the work checked?" — which is the question our criteria operationalize. They also widen the channel's supply: on this reading the stock thought experiments are one species of a general practice in which idealized, deliberately unreal scenarios carry representational load in concept formation [R06] and in modeling [R07]. Both literatures supply the raw material for the assessment below: Norton's reconstruction program is the dispensability check in its strongest form, and the fictionalist frame [R07] explains how a fiction can be a working input while remaining a fiction — representation does not require truth.

### 3.3 The operational definition of "controlled"

We call a fictional construction **controlled** when its epistemic yield is translatable into a checkable argument or model: a derivation that someone can rerun, a limiting case that someone can verify, a model whose behavior can be compared against targets. Checkable means re-derivable by a competent third party from the reconstruction alone — the same re-derivability standard that the reverse channel's adjudication agenda will impose on AI-generated hypotheses in §8, which is one reason the two ends of the loop share criteria.

Two boundary clarifications. Translatability is a property of the yield, not of the imagery: nothing in the criterion requires the fictional dressing to be disposable, which is why the definition does not take sides in §3.1's dispute. And translatability is a threshold, not a quality grade: a scenario whose yield cannot be laid out as argument or model is outside the channel's scope — however inspiring — while one whose yield can be so laid out is inside, whatever its literary texture. Translatability is thus the boundary between controlled fiction and free imagination, and it is the channel's criterion rather than a courtesy paid to one side of the literature: Norton's reconstructions are precisely translations, and Brown's stock cases are precisely scenarios whose yields survive translation.

**Channel claim.** Controlled fiction is a routine production material of science, and the channel criterion is translatability.

**Channel assessment.**

- *T (temporal priority).* The scenario is constructed before the argument it organizes: construction is the fictional step, reconstruction the checkable one, and in the stock cases of the literature the construction is datable and the reconstruction follows it [R04], [R05]. **Pass.**
- *D (dispensability).* This is the disputed check by construction: Norton's position is that the fiction is dispensable, Brown's that it is not. The channel claim does not need the dispute settled. What it claims is production value under translation, not indispensability of the imagery; and if Norton is right, the channel's residue is the translated argument itself, which still passes R. **Unresolved by design; the claim is worded to be invariant under either resolution.**
- *R (operative residue).* The residue is a reusable argumentative and modeling technology — limiting cases, idealizations, reconstruction templates — that remains in routine scientific use after any particular scenario has served. **Pass.**

The channel passes T and R, with D unresolved by design. The invariance of the claim under the Brown–Norton outcome is a feature of the criterion, not a dodge: translatability was chosen because both disputants grant it.

## 4. Channel III: A Fictional Quantity Becomes an Engineering Quantity

### 4.1 Entropy's two journeys

The third channel runs on quantities. A quantity defined over an abstract, deliberately idealized object — an ensemble of messages with no material location — can travel into engineering by formal isomorphism and travel back into physics carrying a measurable price. The fiction here is not a story but a definitional act: the referent of the quantity is an abstract probabilistic object, constructed for the purpose, in the idealization sense of §3.

The stock example is entropy's second life. Statistical mechanics had long given entropy a statistical reading over microstates. Shannon [R09] then defined the information produced by a message source as H = -Σ p·log p — a quantity whose referent is an ensemble of messages, an object with no mass, no location, and no temperature. The definitional act is the fictional step, and it is datable. Jaynes [R08] built the bridge back toward physics: recasting statistical mechanics as inference, he showed that the same maximization principle that governs the informational quantity governs the derivation of physical distributions — the identity of form between the two quantities was made to carry weight rather than to decorate. Landauer [R10] completed the circuit by rematerializing the quantity: the erasure of information, he showed, carries a thermodynamic cost of kT·ln2 per bit, so the abstract measure re-entered physics as an expense with experimental consequences — a price that a physical apparatus pays for a bookkeeping operation on an abstract object.

Each arrow in this circuit is anchored to a datable primary source: the definitional act to Shannon [R09], the bridge to Jaynes [R08], the rematerialization to Landauer [R10]. No arrow rests on recollection, and none requires the others to be read as metaphor: the circuit is made of definitions, derivations, and a cost, all checkable in the primary texts. Each arrow also stands without the others — the isomorphism holds whether or not one emphasizes the bridge, and the rematerialization holds whether or not one accepts the bridge as more than a formal observation — which is why the verdict below is a conjunction of separately anchored facts.

### 4.2 Verdict and channel claim

The channel's verdict rests on three observable facts. The isomorphism of formula: the same expression, H = -Σ p·log p, governs the abstract ensemble and the physical distribution — not a similar expression, the same one. The rematerialization: the quantity acquires an experimental price, kT·ln2 per erased bit [R10], which is what distinguishes rematerialization from a mere change of subject. And the standing of information in physical reasoning today: a quantity with thermodynamic consequences rather than a metaphor.

**Channel claim.** A fictional quantity can enter engineering by isomorphism and flow back into physics at a measurable cost; every arrow in the vocabulary circuit has an anchor.

**Channel assessment.**

- *T (temporal priority).* Each arrow is dated by its primary publication [R08], [R09], [R10]. This is the best-dated of the four channels. **Pass.**
- *D (dispensability).* Whether communication theory would be equally parsimonious without H is decided by nothing in the pool, and we do not assert it. What the pool does show is that the arrows are real and dated; it does not show that no other route could have produced the same theory. The check is indeterminate here, and the channel claim is graded to rest on the anchorability of the arrows, not the indispensability of the quantity — the same supply-level discipline as Channel I. **Indeterminate; graded accordingly.**
- *R (operative residue).* The residue is an operative quantity with units, prices, and experimental tests. **Pass.**

The channel passes T and R strongly; D is indeterminate, and the claim is stated at the anchored-arrow level. The difference between "partial pass" in Channel I and "indeterminate" here is evidential, not rhetorical: in Channel I the pool contains positive evidence of non-uniqueness [R01], so the check can run and downgrade the claim to the supply level, whereas here the pool contains no study of alternative formulations, so the check cannot run at all — and the claim is graded to be invariant under its outcome rather than partially passed. Of the four channels this is the most mechanical, and deliberately so: quantities carry their definitions with them, so the feedback can be exhibited as an identity of formula plus a cost, without appeal to influence, reception, or intention.

## 5. Channel IV: Narrative Fiction into the AI Agenda

### 5.1 A direct agenda-setting case

The cleanest case in this channel is also the most literal. Sagan's novel Contact turns on rapid interstellar travel; the novelist's inquiry to a physicist — whether such travel could be made scientifically respectable — is the documented hinge of the case, and the answer became the published analysis of traversable wormholes by Morris and Thorne [R11]. The passability question was answered as physics: what holds a wormhole open, and what conditions a traveler must meet, are derived and preserved in the journal record, stated as equations rather than as plot.

The evidence structure is what makes the case flagship-grade for this channel. The chain from the novelist's question to the published solution is anchored by the authors' own accounts of the episode (**self-statement anchor**); the scientific content is anchored by the peer-reviewed paper itself (**primary-source anchor**). Narrative-channel evidence usually has one anchor or the other — a story that influenced someone, or a paper whose fictional origin is hearsay. Here both ends of the same chain are anchored, and the two anchor types meet at a datable event. That the two anchors coincide on one case is what makes it the channel's flagship — and also why the case is one case: its strength is evidentiary, and it does not by itself establish a pattern. The case also displays the channel's characteristic evidence problem, and its solution. Influence is the kind of thing that usually leaves recollections and little else, so the channel's standard is that a recollection must meet a published artifact: recollection supplies the fictional end of the chain, the artifact supplies the scientific end, and neither is asked to do the other's work.

### 5.2 Ontological vocabulary

Narrative fiction also supplies the nouns an agenda needs, and vocabulary supply is the channel's most mechanical evidence form, because dictionary records are checkable artifacts. The word robot entered the language through Čapek's play R.U.R. (1920); the Oxford English Dictionary's earliest recorded attestation of robotics falls in Asimov's 1941 story "Liar!" [R12]. These are **lexical anchors**: the record is public, dated, and independent of anyone's recollection, which is what makes it an anchor rather than an anecdote. The vocabulary is not decorative. Robot and robotics are the term of art for an entire artifact class across law, industry, and research, and the term descends, by dictionary record, from the fiction that named it.

The same holds for singularity. Vinge's 1993 essay [R13], published in an interdisciplinary NASA-sponsored volume, states the coming of intelligence beyond the human threshold as a near-term engineering question; the singularity vocabulary in its AI-agenda sense carries this named, datable textual anchor (**lexical anchor**). We make no claim about the word's earlier history — prior travels are exactly what the lexical-anchor method refuses to assert without a record. What the channel needs is that the agenda-bearing sense has an anchor, and it does.

A lexical anchor does double duty in the assessment below: it dates the vocabulary (T), and it exhibits the residue (R), since a term of art still in circulation is an operative residue by definition. Both anchors land in this paper's territory: the robotics vocabulary names and regulates a class of autonomous artifacts, and the singularity vocabulary organizes the discourse about AI trajectories. The narrative channel's residues are, in these two cases, residues inside the discourse this paper is about.

### 5.3 The quantitative tier

Beyond flagship cases and vocabulary, the channel has a quantitative tier: recent studies examine the association between science-fiction narratives and technology topics quantitatively [R14], [R15] (**quantitative-study tier**). The methodological role of this tier is to move the channel from anecdote to measurement: if the association between fictional narratives and technical agendas can be measured over studies rather than recounted in episodes, then the channel's hypothesis — science fiction supplies topics to technology — is testable, and has been tested, at study level. We do not assert the strength or the direction of the association here; the tier's contribution to the claim is its existence and form. The tier also disciplines the rest of the channel: with a measurable form available, the flagship case and the vocabulary are not asked to carry statistical weight, and the quantitative studies are not asked to explain single episodes.

### 5.4 Evidence tiers and the forward pointer

The channel's evidence comes in three grades, each with an anchor. Self-statement anchors: the participants document the fictional origin themselves ([R11]). Lexical anchors: dictionaries and primary texts date the vocabulary ([R12], [R13]). Quantitative studies: the association is measured rather than recounted ([R14], [R15]). The grades are stated separately, and none is asked to carry more than it supports: the flagship case is one case; the vocabulary is supply, not necessity; the quantitative tier measures association, not mechanism. This grading is what allows the channel claim below to be exact about what narrative fiction does — it supplies nouns and it sets agendas — without borrowing strength from the grade above.

**Channel claim.** Narrative fiction both supplies vocabulary and sets agendas; the evidence is graded by anchor type, and every grade has an anchor.

**Channel assessment.**

- *T (temporal priority).* Precedences are datable, and each precedence carries its anchor type with it: the novel's question precedes the wormhole paper (self-statement anchor, [R11]); 1920 and 1941 precede the modern robotics vocabulary (lexical anchor, [R12]); 1993 precedes the discourse the singularity essay anchors (lexical anchor, [R13]). **Pass.**
- *D (dispensability).* Whether the same vocabulary and agenda would have arisen without the fictional sources is decided by nothing in the pool, and we do not assert it. Vocabulary supply is the kind of input that has substitutes; the channel claim does not need indispensability. **Indeterminate; graded accordingly.**
- *R (operative residue).* The residue is operative: a term of art in law, industry, and research; a discourse-organizing concept with a datable anchor; and a class of spacetime solutions entered into the physics literature with published conditions [R11]. **Pass.**

Channel IV is also where the loop between fiction and the sciences stops being purely historical. An engineering-grade instance of the full pipeline — fictional world-building to formal specification to pre-registered adjudication to falsifiable experiment, with negative results disclosed in full — is on record, and it is examined in §7 on the same footing as the cases in this paper: scored by the same three checks, held to the same standards of disclosure, and consolidated into the same table in §9.1. The checks transfer unchanged; what changes is the tier on which the verdict products are graded (§1.2), and §7 is written so that the two tiers remain visibly distinct.

## 6. Method: Porting the Hard Criteria

### 6.1 The definition domain

Hard science fiction is not our coinage, and we do not redefine it. The definition domain is taken from the reference literature: Nicholls's encyclopedia entry [R37], which fixes the term's sense as science fiction distinguished by the care, accuracy, and consequence of its science, and Hartwell and Cramer's anthology framing [R36], which treats hard SF as a tradition organized around scientific content. Within that domain we extract five operational criteria, as a scale for inputs rather than as a definition of the genre: internal consistency — the world's ledger runs without deficit; no supernatural checks — nothing is paid for by powers the world has not established; continuity with known science; precise technical imagination — the imagined device can be specified, and the diegetic-prototype literature shows narrative devices turning into engineering work [R32], [R33]; and checkability from inside the world — an observer within the fiction could, in principle, run the check. The five criteria are deliberately coarse. Their purpose is the mapping below.

### 6.2 The mapping table

The table asserts one invariant, and it is the same question in every row: given the ledger, can a third party recompute the conclusion from the ledger alone? On the right-hand side this recomputability criterion is adopted in its systematized form from a companion audit-theoretic study of AI reasoning claims, withheld during review under double-blind discipline and restored at camera-ready. The table asserts structural isomorphism between the two columns; it is not an equivalence theorem, and nothing downstream depends on it being one.

| # | Hard-SF criterion | AI-research counterpart | Common failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Internal consistency: the world's ledger runs without deficit [R37] | Claim and evidence structure agree [R38] | Self-contradiction |
| M2 | No supernatural checks: nothing is paid for by unestablished powers [R36] | No appeal to undelivered future capability [R39] | Deus ex machina |
| M3 | Continuity with known science [R36] | Anchored to existing re-checkable artifacts [R38] | Assertion from nothing |
| M4 | Narrative objects can be commissioned by engineering: diegetic prototypes with documented effects [R32], [R33] | Adjudication procedure pre-registered, third-party recomputable [R38], [R39] | Changing the rules after the fact |
| M5 | Checkable by an observer inside the world [R37] | Falsifiable — and actually exposed to being falsified: the kill line precedes the run [R38], [R39] | Unfalsifiable rhetoric |

The discipline of borrowing the definition domain rather than coining one is not pedantry. A self-made definition of hard SF could make the mapping fail silently: if the left column were tailored to the right, the isomorphism would be bought, not found. Fixing the left column to [R36] and [R37] keeps the port honest — the mapping has to work on criteria the fiction side already owns.

Two reading notes. The left column is a scale for fictional inputs; the right column is a scale for research claims; the middle of the table is where a fiction-to-science channel either acquires adjudication or remains a mood. And the rows are ordered by increasing strictness: M1–M3 can be checked by reading, M4–M5 require procedure — which is why the pipeline below exists.

One row worked through, as an example of the table's granularity. M4's left cell says narrative objects can be commissioned by engineering, and the diegetic-prototype studies document it: fictional devices from productions moving into laboratories as artifacts with budgets and test plans [R32], [R33]. M4's right cell says adjudication procedures are pre-registered and third-party recomputable. The shared failure mode is rule-changing after the fact — the studio re-edits the ending; the lab redefines the metric. What makes the row one row is not surface similarity between films and pipelines but the invariant above: in both cells, the question is whether a third party holding the ledger alone would reach the same verdict.

### 6.3 Existing base and stopping point

The idea that narrative objects can steer real research and development is not new, and we do not claim it. Science-fiction prototyping [R30], design fiction [R31], and the diegetic-prototype line [R32], [R33] document films, prototypes, and speculative designs entering laboratories and product programs; Dourish and Bell [R34] analyze how such imaginaries structure research agendas in ubiquitous computing; Blythe [R35] shows fictional abstracts functioning as research instruments in HCI. The lineage's stopping point is equally documented: its home venues are HCI and STS, its outputs are prototypes and provocations, and its connection to the methodology's own control instruments in machine learning — reproducibility standards [R38] and the trend critiques [R39] — has not been drawn. That the ML side already has instruments for a related anxiety makes the gap sharper, not smaller: checklists and failure-mode taxonomies are controls on claims, but they are not a port of the hard-SF criteria, and nothing in them tells a researcher what to do with a fictional input. The gap this paper occupies is that connection: porting the hard criteria into AI-research methodology through a pipeline whose stages can be frozen and audited. To the extent of our three-line search completed on 2026-09-16, the port does not exist in the literature; a second-round novelty check is scheduled before submission, and this statement carries that gate, as in §1.3.

### 6.4 The four-stage pipeline

| Stage | Input | Product | Criteria | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S: setting | Narrative fiction | Self-consistent world setting plus ontological vocabulary | M1 | Hidden contradiction |
| F: specification | S | Formal system; identity ledger | M2, M3 | Bare metaphor — no formal counterpart |
| P: pre-registration | F | Frozen adjudication functions plus external anchors | M4 | Changing the rules after the fact |
| E: experiment | P | Verdicts, including kills | M5 | Unfalsifiable rhetoric |

The stages are sequential and the products are cumulative, but the load-bearing stage is P. A kill line must be a property of the frozen product, not of the experimenter's judgment: a threshold written before the run, a verdict function that any third party can rerun on the same inputs, and an anchor — a content hash — that fixes function and inputs together. The anchor is what makes the freeze portable: a hash published before the run lets any later reader verify that the function which judged the data is the function that was written. Freezing without anchoring is a promise; anchoring without freezing is a hash of nothing. If a stage cannot name its kill lines, the pipeline has not been built; it has been narrated. §7 walks one artifact through all four stages, and the walk, not this section, is the portability argument.

**Method claim.** The hard criteria and the falsifiability criteria of AI research claims are structurally isomorphic under M1–M5, and the four-stage pipeline is a portable methodology; portability is claimed because §7 executes it, not because this section describes it.

## 7. The Case: One Artifact Through the Full Pipeline

One engineering-grade, mid-strength instance of the full pipeline is on record; we present it on the same footing as the cases above, scored by the same criteria, with its negative results disclosed in full. During review the artifact bundle is referenced through an anonymized pointer (Availability); the frozen file names quoted below are part of the evidence and are reproduced verbatim.

### 7.1 Chain replay

**S.** The input is a fictional condensed-matter setting: a world in which reasoning paths across a concept-decomposition graph are carried by a fictional particle species bound to each node, and every traversal is a three-channel scattering event — transmission, reflection, and irreversible dissipation — governed by an exact bookkeeping identity, T+R+A=1, where T, R, A are the three channel shares of the traversing unit. The fiction supplies what Channel IV predicts it should: an ontological vocabulary (states, scattering channels) and a pledge — the identity — precise enough to be betrayed.

**F.** Specification binds the vocabulary to formal objects: each node holds a two-parameter state on a directed weighted graph; the reverse-dynamics update is an explicit projection-composition operator; the candidate potential Φ and the three-channel ledger are defined so that the identity is checkable per path. Two formal products matter. The identity ledger turns the fictional physics' promise into an exact bookkeeping constraint on the implementation. And the game-theoretic formalization — a frozen specification whose SHA-256 prefix is aeefb8ef6972 — models the reverse dynamics as a potential game on the graph and states three propositions with kill lines: P1, dynamic equivalence to best response, in a strong best-response form (P1a), a conditional form (P1b), and a first-order entropy-regularized form (P1c); P2, potential completeness; P3, a commitment-device reading of the dissipation channel. The specification also defines the empirical coordination ratio (ECR) — the artifact's term for the coordination premium of the field arm over selfish arms, whose frozen fields retain the legacy name `GT4_price_of_anarchy` — used to quantify coordination value.

**P.** Pre-registration freezes adjudication before any data exist. Verdict pure functions are committed and test-locked; kill lines are written into the frozen specification (a direction cosine below zero decides P1b; sup-norm deviations beyond stated bounds decide P1a and P3; a violation-fraction beyond one third downgrades P2); and the specification and run scripts are hash-anchored (SHA-256, 12-hex prefixes: specification aeefb8ef6972, v21 run script 9bbe43f41fa8, v22 run script 6e9673205dc0). The experiments issue no API calls — purely seeded local computation, seed 210021 — so recomputation is mechanical, and the frozen specification's receipt records that reruns reproduce the result files byte-for-byte.

**E.** Execution runs the frozen functions on frozen inputs. Scale: 61 systematically sampled graphs (n ≤ 8; chains, stars, trees, random DAGs, cyclic), 338 full-candidate-mask tasks, 6,760 enumerated kill-check states; the sampling caliber is disclosed in the artifact's own honesty fields — systematic sampling of graph families, not 2^(n²) enumeration. The verdicts, each traceable to a frozen field path, are:

- *The identity holds.* Maximum per-path audit deviation of T+R+A from 1 is 2.220446049250313e-16, double-precision machine epsilon (deposon_v19_benchmark_fixes.json: physics_audit.t_plus_r_plus_a_max_deviation).
- *P1a, killed.* The maximum deviation between the artifact's update operator and the best-response operator, in infinity norm, is 0.8569 (deposon_v21_gtformal.json: verdict.P1a_deviation.max_dev_inf = 0.8568983495366105).
- *P1b, killed.* The direction cosine between the update direction and the best-response direction reaches −1.0 — the kill reason is literally cosine<0; the minimum cosine is −1.0000000000000002 and the minimum potential gain along a trajectory is −1.2424e−2 (deposon_v21_gtformal.json: verdict.T_P1b.min_cosine, .min_dPhi).
- *P1c, killed twice.* Under a frozen τ grid, both the strong global-τ form and the weak state-dependent form die; the best achievable cosine is still −1.0 (deposon_v22_p1c.json: verdict.strong_global_tau = killed, verdict.weak_state_dependent = killed, verdict.verdict = killed, min_cos_at_best_global_tau = −1.0000000000000002).
- *P2, downgraded.* Cyclic-support graphs exceed the pre-registered residual bound on 92.4% of tasks (frac_cyclic_high = 0.9238 > 1/3), which triggers the pre-registered downgrade to approximate potential game; the 10 acyclic-support tasks pass (n_dag = 10, n_cyclic = 328) (deposon_v21_gtformal.json: verdict.T_P2.*).
- *P3, split.* The payment-reparametrization equivalence dies (max |r(0.1) − r(0)| = 0.1585), while the structure-change reading survives — dissipation genuinely moves the best-response fixed points (max difference 0.8442) (deposon_v21_gtformal.json: verdict.T_P3.reparametrization_claim = killed, structure_change_claim = survives, .max_r_diff, .max_fp_diff).
- *Coordination value, supported at consistency level.* ECR median 1.333 over the 17 finite per-graph values, verdict field_coordination_value_supported (deposon_v20_gt.json: GT4_price_of_anarchy.verdict.median_poa, .poa_per_graph_finite).

Caliber disclosures travel with the verdicts, as the pipeline requires: the residual ratio is a norm ratio where an earlier run used a squared ratio, and an implementation bug in the acyclicity criterion was found, fixed, and test-locked before the runs whose verdicts are quoted above.

### 7.2 Kills as hardness

The point of rehearsing seven verdicts is their distribution. The three kills — P1a, P1b, and P1c in both its forms — are the pipeline's operational evidence of hardness: a pipeline that could do nothing but confirm would be a narrative with hashes on it. What makes the artifact engineering-grade is that its propositions were stated sharply enough to die publicly, by functions frozen before the run, and that the deaths are recorded as results; the artifact's own conclusion is that its strong dynamic-equivalence claims are dead and that any future claim of the same family must pass the frozen kill lines before it is made. Negative results here are not a wrinkle to be explained away. They are the asset (M5), which is the hard-SF criterion doing exactly what it does in a well-run fictional ledger: the world's account does not cover itself.

### 7.3 Honest positioning

*Channel.* The loop this case closes is the audit and epistemics channel: the fiction produced an accountable formal object and a verdict discipline, not a capability gain. The capability channel is zero, and the artifact's own experiments say so. On real benchmarks the scattering layer is statistically indistinguishable from a trivial rule filter over label strings: GSM8K 0.87 versus 0.85, 95% CI [−11.8, +7.8] pp, discordant pairs (b, c) = (0, 2), detectable threshold approximately ±10 pp; StrategyQA 0.899 versus 0.899, CI [−8.8, +8.8] pp (deposon_v22_e95ci.json: gsm8k, strategyqa). Chain-of-thought prompting significantly outperforms the artifact's arm: 0.97 versus 0.85, 95% CI [−20.5, −4.1] pp — the interval excludes zero (deposon_v22_e95ci.json: unified_vs_cot). And convex fusion with a semantic prior dilutes the prior instead of helping it: physics graph 0.484 → 0.452, historical graph 0.783 → 0.739 at λ = 0.5 (deposon_v20_crossval.json: prior_arm_eval fields at hybrid_lambda_convex = 0.5). We report these as they stand.

*Magnitude.* The case is structurally isomorphic to Channel I — a fictional vocabulary became a formal system that then constrained its own claims — and magnitude-incomparable to it. Sixty-one graphs and three killed propositions are not non-Euclidean geometry. The isomorphism is of structure, not of scale, and nothing in this paper reads it otherwise.

*Unfinished.* The non-Euclidean-grade loop of Channel I required third-party adoption: physicists with no stake in the geometry picked the language up. The case has audit-grade closure but no third-party adoption; by this paper's own criteria its loop is closed to the level of internal recomputability, and no further. What would upgrade it is an external group freezing their own verdict functions against the same fictional input — the agenda of §8.3, one instance earlier.

Scored by the three checks of §1.2 and the mapping of §6, whose lineage is fixed in §6.1–§6.3 ([R30], [R37], [R38], [R39]): *T* passes with stage-level anchoring — the fictional setting precedes the specification, and the pipeline's own frozen stages date the precedence; *D* is indeterminate and disclosed as such — the fictional bookkeeping was the generative source of the identity and of the propositions, but the artifact contains no fiction-free reconstruction, so indispensability is neither established nor claimed; *R* passes — the residue is operative: frozen specifications, kill lines, and verdicts that now bind future claims of the same family. ## 8. Outlook: The Reverse Channel

*This section is an open-question agenda. It is not an empirical claim, and it makes no predictions. Its deliverable is the adjudication design that would let the question die.*

### 8.1 The existence line

The forward channels of §2–§5 ran on human fiction. The reverse question asks whether the loop now has a second driver: whether systems that build internal world models — machine worldviews — generate inputs to the exact sciences in the same decidable sense. The term is used at the grain the definitional debate permits: the survey literature on world models spans a family of senses — learned simulators, latent dynamics models, generative environments — whose boundary is actively contested [R20]. We add no definition and design the agenda to be insensitive to which sense wins.

The existence line for the ingredients is real. World models as learned internal simulators go back to Ha and Schmidhuber's agents acting inside their own learned representations of environments [R16]; DreamerV3 made one family of them — recurrent state-space models trained on imagined rollouts — general across domains [R17]; Genie and Cosmos extend the line to generative interactive environments learned from video and to world-foundation models aimed at physical systems [R18], [R19]. A sequence model trained on Othello move sequences develops internally probeable board representations — a machine worldview in miniature, with structure the training data did not write in [R21]. LeCun's programmatic proposal places world models at the core of autonomous machine intelligence, with prediction of the world as the central objective [R22]. The ingredients exist; that is the existence line, and it is all this section claims.

For the agenda's purposes the definitional debate does not have to be settled. What the three entry conditions below need is a functional stand-in: a system whose behavior is organized by a learned model of an environment's structure — probeable, or plannable-with, at whatever grain — regardless of where the definitional debate eventually draws its boundaries [R20]. The entry conditions do not mention the word worldview at all, and that is deliberate: the agenda's verdicts attach to artifacts — specifications, verdict functions, kill lines — not to contested concepts.

### 8.2 The pipeline line

On the production side, the stages of §6.4 have early machine analogs. The AI Scientist generates and executes research ideas end to end, and its successor extends the line toward workshop-level papers [R23], [R24]; Coscientist plans and executes chemical synthesis against automated laboratories [R25]; AI co-scientist organizes multi-agent hypothesis generation [R26]; Agent Laboratory automates the loop from literature review to experimental code [R27]; and blind human review has judged LLM-generated research ideas more novel than expert-written ones [R28]. Read against §6.4, these systems already occupy the S stage and parts of the F stage: they produce settings and specifications.

What, to the extent of our three-line search completed on 2026-09-16, none of them yet does is submit its products to a P stage — frozen adjudication functions with kill lines written before evaluation, by a party other than the generating system. The statement carries the second-round check gate. The distinction this paper draws is disciplinary, not pejorative. These systems are optimized to produce outputs; the pipeline requires outputs that can be killed. An S-stage product without a P stage is a proposal, and the forward channels' own history says what turns proposals into inputs: Morris and Thorne answered a novelist's question with a paper whose criteria were checkable (§5.1); the case of §7 froze its verdict functions before its runs. The machine analog is exact: a generated hypothesis compiled into a specification whose kill lines are frozen before the hypothesis's own evaluation — by someone other than the generator.

### 8.3 The adjudication agenda

The reverse channel becomes decidable when three conditions hold; they are §6.4's pipeline read as entry criteria. *Freezability*: the machine-generated hypothesis can be compiled into a formal specification whose functions and inputs can be hashed (F→P). *Pre-registrability*: verdict functions and kill lines can be frozen before evaluation, by a party other than the generating system (P). *Executability*: a decisive test exists at affordable cost — the kill line can actually be run (E). A machine worldview counts as opening the reverse channel, in this paper's operational sense, when its outputs pass all three and then either survive or die by them. If AI-generated hypotheses, run under such pre-registered adjudication, systematically leave no kill line to hang — no falsifiable consequence that a decisive test can reject — the reverse channel is declared dead (F-C3, §1.2). The audit of such runs has a starting point: the failure-mode taxonomy of AI-assisted science [R29], which enumerates how computational rigor breaks in practice. The ledger-theoretic toolkit for running the audit — recomputability as a property of claims — is systematized in the companion study withheld during review (interface as in §6.2).

The conditions inherit the pipeline's failure modes. Freezability fails when the hypothesis is prose alone — a setting that cannot be compiled gives the agenda nothing to freeze, the F-stage failure of bare metaphor. Pre-registrability fails when the generating system also writes the verdict functions — the rules were changed before the run began. Executability fails when the kill line exists on paper and no affordable test can reach it — unfalsifiable rhetoric in expensive clothing. Each failure is itself observable, which makes the design falsifiable twice over: its subject can die, and so can the claim that the subject was ever eligible.

Three outcomes are possible, and the agenda treats them symmetrically. If machine-generated hypotheses pass the three conditions and survive their kill lines, the reverse channel has an anchored case, at that case's own strength, to be recorded in §9.1's table like any other row. If they pass and die, the reverse channel has a pipeline and no payload — the audit-and-epistemics channel of §7.3, now with a machine author. If they systematically never reach the conditions — no kill line to hang — the reverse channel is declared dead (F-C3). The design borrows its honesty from §7: the interesting outcome is not which verdict arrives, but that a verdict has become schedulable.

The agenda is deliberately modest. It does not assert that machine worldviews feed back into science; it makes the assertion decidable.

### 9.2 Limitations

Four limitations bound the paper. *Selection effect*: this is a typology of detectable feedback — channels whose fictional inputs left no anchor are invisible to the method by construction, and the four forms partition the cases we could anchor, not all feedback. *Case scale*: one engineering-grade instance; its kills are evidence about the pipeline, not about the channels' magnitude. *Magnitude*: structural isomorphism between the case and Channel I is not magnitude equivalence, and we have not extrapolated it. *Two tiers*: the historical evidence tier and the pre-registered adjudication tier are different instruments; nothing in §7 retroactively strengthens the historical sections, and nothing in §2–§5 borrows the verdict discipline of §7.

### 9.3 What would weaken this paper

F-C1–F-C4, restated as the closing gate. The typology weakens if the four forms become pairwise inseparable under the three checks. A channel downgrades if its flagship case's fictional input is shown dispensable or its precedence fails. The reverse channel dies if AI-generated hypotheses, run under pre-registered adjudication, systematically leave no kill line to hang. The whole apparatus is ad hoc if new cases cannot be classified by the same criteria without ambiguity. Each is a finding someone could produce, and the paper is built so that each would be visible.

None of these limitations is fatal, and none is decorative: each one is load-bearing for a claim level chosen above. The typology claims to partition anchored cases, not all feedback; the case claims executability, not capability; the isomorphism claims structure, not scale; and the two-tier discipline claims nothing beyond what each tier's instruments can measure.

Fiction is not evidence. But its feedback into the exact sciences is recurrent, anchorable, and decidable — and, under the same instruments, the question of a reverse channel has stopped being a mood and become a schedule.
