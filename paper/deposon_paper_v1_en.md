## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) remain vulnerable to surface-association traps in multi-step reasoning: when a problem statement contains numerical cues that are superficially related but semantically irrelevant to the correct solution, greedy path selection is systematically deflected toward decoy branches. We present Deposon, a physics-constrained layer that maps an LLM-generated concept graph onto a collection of node-bound scattering centers and subjects candidate reasoning paths to a three-channel scattering process — transmission, reflection, and irreversible dissipation into a formal "ether" reservoir (a purely terminological borrowing; no physical-ontology claim is made). The two behaviors of earlier designs, blocking (v1) and tunneling (v2), are shown to be two limit regimes of a single two-parameter family; the scattering weights are a constructive definition (satisfying the exact identity $T+R+A=1$ for all parameter values) motivated by a formal analogy with Feshbach-resonance-style effective $S$-matrices. On two controlled synthetic benchmarks (100 simple arithmetic items and 100 surface-association trap items; seed=42; concept graphs produced by a real LLM backend with zero rule-based fallbacks in the final run), the unified variant raises accuracy from 7% (simple) and 10% (trap) for the greedy no-Deposon baseline to 100% on both sets — within-graph selection effect sizes of +0.93 and +0.90 — and reaches 100% on all six categories of our trap taxonomy. A system-wide physical audit reports a maximum unitarity deviation of $2.2\times10^{-16}$ (machine precision), and limit-regime dissipation rates (0% / 86.92% / 8.63%) that match the theoretical predictions. We additionally specify a fixed protocol for a controlled comparison against chain-of-thought baselines on a real GSM8K subset. Final results (primary protocol, pre-committed before the experiment): on the real GSM8K subset, CoT reaches 97.0% and Deposon-unified 85.0% (McNemar $p=4.9\times10^{-4}$, CoT significantly better) — the bidirectional interpretation framework pre-committed in Sec. 4.2 is resolved in its "information-loss cost dominates" outcome, and we report this without embellishment. We emphasize throughout that the reported effect sizes measure the *increment of path selection within a fixed concept graph*, not an improvement of the LLM's intrinsic reasoning capability. Two further controls sharpen this boundary: a label-shuffle ablation (5 seeds) collapses trap-benchmark accuracy from 100% to 17.2%±6.4%, and a uniform-params control to 10% — the layer is a *transducer* of semantic labels into auditable energy decisions, not an independent discriminator. We also disclose that the no_deposon baseline is decoy-baited by graph construction (decoy edges weighted 0.9 vs 0.6); against the neutral uniform-params control (which coincides numerically with the greedy baseline on both sets) the increments are +0.93 (simple) and +0.90 (trap). Two further algorithmic ablations sharpen the picture: the label dependence can be removed *at scoring time* (labelfree computes the binding from structural signals without reading type strings — 100% on both benchmarks, 25/25 bit-identical under label shuffling); however, in the current pipeline these structural signals are themselves transcribed from type labels (label→weight), so genuine label-information independence remains to be verified with weights from an independent source — the residual dependence is honestly disclosed; and the dormant resonance channel can be activated losslessly (resonant/resonant_hybrid both 100%). Finally, we pre-commit to a bidirectional interpretation framework for the real-benchmark comparison (Sec. 4.2): whether the constraint layer exhibits anti-capture gains or information-loss costs on real tasks, both outcomes fall within the same framework — anti-capture gains and information-loss costs together form the complete scientific profile of an auditable constraint layer. This boundary, together with an honest account of verifier false positives, is treated as a first-class part of the contribution.

**Keywords:** physics-inspired reasoning; energy dissipation; Feshbach resonance; large language models; reasoning path selection; irreversibility; auditability

## 1 Introduction

Large language models have achieved striking progress on multi-step reasoning tasks such as math word problems, commonsense inference, and code generation [1, 2, 11]. Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting [1] elicits intermediate reasoning steps and substantially improves arithmetic and symbolic reasoning; Tree of Thoughts (ToT) [3] and Self-Consistency (SC) [5] generalize single-chain reasoning into search and voting, respectively. Taken together, these results suggest that the reasoning quality of an LLM depends not only on model scale but, critically, on **how its intermediate reasoning paths are organized**.

Multi-step reasoning reliability is nevertheless limited by three structural problems. First, *surface-association traps*: numbers and keywords in the problem text that are superficially related but semantically irrelevant to the correct solution (e.g., the word "altogether" suggesting addition when the correct operation is subtraction) systematically attract LLMs and their search-based derivatives toward decoy branches [36, 37]. Second, *error compounding along chains*: in CoT/ToT-style methods, an erroneous intermediate step carries no intrinsic cost, so descendants of an error node continue to consume the search budget. Third, *uninterpretability of path selection*: voting and heuristic scoring cannot answer the structural question of *why* a given path should be eliminated, and therefore cannot be audited.

More fundamentally, existing methods eliminate wrong paths *reversibly*: a pruned branch can be revived at the next expansion, the next sample, or the next prompt. This stands in contrast to the physical world — in open quantum systems, once energy dissipates into an environment in the thermodynamic-limit idealization of infinitely many degrees of freedom, the Poincaré recurrence time diverges and dissipation is irreversible for all practical purposes [30, 31]. Our position is that such intrinsic properties of physical systems — irreversible dissipation, resonance enhancement, and unitarity constraints — are not decorative metaphors but structural constraints that can be built directly into an algorithm's cost and conservation structure, essentially for free. Throughout this paper, every physical correspondence is stated as a **formal mapping with an explicit mathematical boundary**; we make no claim that the algorithm instantiates any real physical process.

We formalize this intuition as **Deposon**, named after *deposition*: the phase transition in which energy passes directly from a gaseous to a solid phase, dispersing irreversibly into an (idealized) infinite-dimensional orthogonal reservoir. Concretely, an LLM first decomposes a problem into a concept graph whose nodes are numbers, operations, traps, and answer candidates; each node is bound to a *deposon state* characterized by two parameters — a coupling strength $g_{\text{couple}}$ to reasoning paths and a coupling strength $g_{\text{aether}}$ to the reservoir. A reasoning path is treated as a stream of "photon" amplitude traversing the deposon field, undergoing three-channel scattering at each node: transmission ($T$), reflection ($R$), and irreversible dissipation into the reservoir ($A$), with the strict identity $T+R+A=1$. Blocking (v1: $g_{\text{aether}}=0$, wrong paths are locally reflected and attenuated) and tunneling (v2: $g_{\text{aether}}\gg0$, erroneous energy is deposited into the reservoir while the correct path transmits nearly losslessly) thereby emerge as two limit regimes of a single entity rather than two separate mechanisms.

We evaluate five ablation variants on two controlled benchmarks of 100 items each (seed=42), with concept graphs produced by a real LLM backend and zero rule-based fallbacks in the final run. As shown in Tables 2 and 3, the unified variant reaches 100% accuracy on both the simple and trap benchmarks, whereas the greedy no-Deposon baseline on the *same* concept graphs reaches only 7% and 10%. The physical audit shows a maximum unitarity deviation of $2.2\times10^{-16}$ across all paths, and the limit-regime energy allocations (v1 dissipation 0%, v2 dissipation 86.92%, unified dissipation 8.63%) match the theoretical predictions.

Our contributions are as follows.

- **C1 — A unified-limits formulation.** We formalize v1/v2 as two limit regimes of a single deposon state (at $g_{\text{aether}}=0$ and $g_{\text{aether}}\gg 0$), give normalized three-channel scattering formulas and a Feshbach-resonance-style effective $S$-matrix, and prove per-path energy conservation (Sec. 3.1, 3.3). All physics correspondences are stated as formal mappings, with the mapping boundary made explicit.
- **C2 — The DeposonField algorithm and system.** A complete pipeline — LLM concept decomposition, deposon field instantiation, BFS path generation, three-channel scattering-based screening, and answer computation along surviving paths — including an irreversible EtherChannel and per-path energy-flow auditing; vectorized scattering over 1,000 deposons takes under 0.2 ms (Sec. 3.2, Appendix C).
- **C3 — A root-cause analysis of zero effect sizes.** We identify and repair two ablation failure modes that made the measured effect size identically zero — decoupling answer computation from the physical screening, and making decoy trap paths unreachable — providing reusable ablation-design lessons for "physics-layer augmentation" research (Sec. 4.1, 5.1).
- **C4 — A controlled-benchmark protocol.** A fixed protocol (and statistical testing plan) for comparing Deposon against CoT baselines on a real GSM8K subset ($n=100$, seed=42); results are pending and clearly marked (Sec. 4.2).

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews related work along four threads; Section 3 presents the method; Section 4 reports experiments; Section 5 discusses limitations, ethics, and a hardware-isomorphism outlook; Section 6 concludes.

## 2 Related Work

We organize the neighboring landscape into four threads, closing each with an explicit statement of how Deposon differs. Our reviewing lens is *structural* rather than empirical: for each family of methods, we ask how a wrong reasoning path is eliminated, whether the elimination mechanism is interpretable, and whether it is reversible.

**(a) LLM reasoning enhancement: from single chains to search — but without conservation constraints.** Chain-of-thought prompting [1] elicits multi-step reasoning by explicitly unrolling intermediate steps, and a zero-shot variant [2] shows the ability can be triggered by a simple instruction; the structural gap is that a single unrolled chain has no error-absorption mechanism — once an intermediate step is wrong, the error compounds along the chain and the injection point cannot be located afterwards. Tree of Thoughts [3] and Graph of Thoughts [4] organize reasoning as tree/graph search with backtracking and branch aggregation, yet node evaluation relies on LLM self-assessment heuristics, and *the search space contains no conserved quantity*: a discarded branch pays no irrecoverable cost, can be revived at the next expansion, and the framework offers no structural answer to why a path should be eliminated. Self-Consistency [5] suppresses random errors by majority voting over sampled chains; its gap is that voting is statistical rather than causal — when errors are *systematic* (e.g., surface-association traps skew the majority of samples in the same direction), the majority fails collectively, and the mechanism provides no interpretable evidence of blocking. Perturbation benchmarks quantify this brittleness directly: on GSM-Symbolic, appending a single irrelevant clause degrades frontier-model accuracy by up to 65 percentage points (the GSM-NoOp subset) [36], and "Alice in Wonderland"-style template problems expose similar systematic failures [37]. ReAct [6] interleaves reasoning with acting for interactive tasks; self-verification [7] and Chain-of-Verification [8] let a model inspect its own output, but verifier and generator share the same model, creating a circularity risk. Process supervision [9] (step-by-step verification) and deductive verification [10] (formalizing CoT and checking it premise by premise) push verification granularity to the step level and are the closest works to ours; however, their "verification" remains a learned or procedural judgment with no underlying constraint tied to the reasoning process, so verifier false positives and negatives cannot be backstopped by any conservation law. **Positioning of Deposon.** We replace none of the above search or sampling mechanisms; instead, we impose on their generated candidate-path space a physical constraint layer with a conservation law ($T+R+A=1$), irreversible dissipation, and per-path energy auditing — the energy budget of an eliminated path cannot flow back, and every blocking decision leaves a re-checkable energy ledger.

**(b) Energy-based models and the Hopfield lineage: static landscapes without an irreversible dissipation channel.** Energy-based models (EBMs) characterize the probability of a configuration by a scalar energy function [13]; their long-standing difficulties are the intractable partition function and MCMC-dependent sampling, and energy there is a *static landscape defining a distribution* rather than a physical quantity that flows and is allocated along a reasoning process. Hopfield networks [14] showed that descent dynamics on an energy landscape implements associative memory; dense generalizations [15] and modern Hopfield networks [16] further established a formal correspondence with Transformer attention. The goal of such dynamics, however, is *convergence to attractors* (retrieving stored patterns), not auditable screening among candidate paths, and the evolution remains within a finite-dimensional state space — there is no channel through which energy, once departed, cannot return. Early formulations of diffusion models [17] explicitly exploited non-equilibrium thermodynamics: a forward process irreversibly destroys structure while a learned reverse process reconstructs it; DDPM [18] developed this into a high-quality generative framework. Irreversibility in diffusion serves *generation* (turning noise into samples), not *constraint* (permanently removing wrong paths). **Positioning of Deposon.** Energy flows along reasoning paths and is allocated across three channels $T/R/A$, of which the dissipation channel leads into a formal infinite-dimensional reservoir where Poincaré recurrence fails (Sec. 3.3); we do not learn an energy landscape — we use conservation and irreversible dissipation as structural primitives for reasoning screening.

**(c) Physics-inspired AI paradigms: soft constraints, closed systems, and hardware isomorphism.** Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) [19] and their field survey [20] inject physical laws into neural network training as soft penalty terms in the loss, pioneering "physics-constrained learning"; the constraints, however, are *soft* — residuals are suppressed by gradients but not guaranteed by identities, conservation laws can still be violated at inference time, and penalty weights must be tuned per task. Hamiltonian neural networks [21] and Lagrangian neural networks [22] achieve *hard* structural constraints by learning a Hamiltonian/Lagrangian, making the model intrinsically respect energy conservation; yet they model closed conservative systems — there is no open-system dissipation channel — and they address continuous dynamics rather than discrete reasoning paths. Photonic computing represents the opposite pole: coherent nanophotonic circuits [23] perform matrix multiplication at the speed of light with Mach–Zehnder interferometer (MZI) meshes, diffractive deep neural networks (D²NN) [35] show that free-space optics alone can carry out inference, and deep optics [24], a neuromorphic-photonics review [25], and a physics-of-optical-computing perspective [26] collectively argue for the hardware isomorphism "interference is computation" — intrinsic physical properties (superposition, interference, loss) can carry computational primitives *for free* instead of being simulated in software. **Positioning of Deposon.** We sit between these two poles: at the algorithmic level, we realize hard constraints via a scattering identity (audited at machine precision, $2.2\times10^{-16}$) and explicitly include an open-system dissipation channel; at the roadmap level, an explicit mapping from the three parameters $(g_{\text{couple}}, g_{\text{aether}}, \Gamma_{\text{aether}})$ to PCM/MZI/ECM device controls (Sec. 5.3) preserves an isomorphic path toward photonic/in-memory hardware. This mapping — stated formally and without any claim of a physical realization — is precisely the criterion separating "physics-inspired" from "physics-metaphorical."

**(d) Neuro-symbolic reasoning and verification: auditable pipelines without a lower-level backstop.** Neuro-symbolic computing advocates principled integration of differentiable learning with symbolic reasoning [32]; the Neuro-Symbolic Concept Learner [33] demonstrated strong compositional generalization in visual question answering via "neural perception + symbolic program execution," validating the value of division-of-labor architectures. On the pure-LLM side, step-level verification (process rewards [9], deductive verification [10]) is moving "verification" from post-hoc scoring to an endogenous stage of reasoning. The shared gap of this thread is that the judgments of the symbolic/verification layer are themselves backed by no lower-level constraint: a symbolic executor can execute wrong premises, and a verifier can false-positive a correct reasoning chain (Sec. 4.4 honestly records the 14%/22% false-positive rates of our own LLM verification layer). **Positioning of Deposon.** Architecturally we are neuro-symbolic — the LLM provides the "neural" part (concept decomposition into a graph) and the deposon field provides the "symbolic-physical" part (constraint propagation on the graph); unlike prior work, we add a lower-level physical backstop beneath the symbolic layer: even if decomposition and verification both err, energy conservation and irreversible dissipation still hold for every path and can be audited independently. Verification disagreements are not suppressed but retained as audit signals.

**Summary.** Each thread solves part of the reasoning-reliability problem — search width (a), energy formalism (b), structural constraints (c), auditability (d) — yet none simultaneously possesses all three elements of *conservation law + irreversible elimination + per-path auditing*. The contribution claim of Deposon is that these three elements can be obtained for free as consequences of a *single formal object* (the deposon state with its two limit regimes), rather than assembled from three independent modules.

## 3 Method

We first present the core formalism of the deposon parameterization (Sec. 3.1), then describe the system architecture embedding it into an LLM reasoning pipeline (Sec. 3.2), then derive energy-flow conservation and argue the irreversibility of reservoir dissipation (Sec. 3.3), and finally define the five-variant ablation design (Sec. 3.4). Full derivations appear in Appendix A.

### 3.1 The deposon parameterization and its two limit regimes

**The unified-limits proposition.** Let $G=(V,E)$ be a concept graph. Each node $v\in V$ is bound to a *deposon state* characterized by a triple: a path-coupling strength $g_{\text{couple}}\ge 0$, a reservoir-coupling strength $g_{\text{aether}}\ge 0$, and a resonance energy $E_0$. A reasoning path ("photon") carrying energy $E_{\text{photon}}$ undergoes three-channel scattering when traversing a node. Then:

- **v1 (blocking) limit** — $g_{\text{aether}}=0$: energy is allocated only between transmission and reflection, $E_{\text{in}}=E_{\text{reflected}}+E_{\text{transmitted}}$, and wrong paths are locally reflected and attenuated;
- **v2 (tunneling) limit** — $g_{\text{aether}}\gg 0$: $E_{\text{in}}=E_{\text{transmitted}}+E_{\text{aether}}$; erroneous energy is deposited into the reservoir while the correct path transmits nearly losslessly;
- **general regime** — $0<g_{\text{aether}}<\infty$: all three channels are open, and behavior interpolates continuously with the ratio $\eta=g_{\text{aether}}/g_{\text{couple}}$.

v1 and v2 are therefore not two mechanisms but *two limit regimes of a single deposon entity on the boundary of its parameter space*; this is the entire content of the "unified" proposition, and its testable corollary is that the energy allocations at the three limit parameter points must exhibit qualitatively consistent limit behavior (verified in Sec. 4.3; a continuous sweep of the ratio $\eta$ is left to future work).

**Resonance-enhanced effective coupling.** Define the detuning as the absolute difference between path energy and the node's resonance energy:

$$\delta = \lvert E_{\text{photon}} - E_0 \rvert, \qquad g_{\text{eff}} = \frac{g_{\text{couple}}}{1+\delta^{2}}. \tag{1}$$

The Lorentzian factor $\frac{1}{1+\delta^2}$ in Eq. (1) is the resonance lineshape: when the path and the node's semantics "resonate" ($\delta\to 0$), the effective coupling reaches its maximum $g_{\text{couple}}$; far off resonance, the constraint relaxes automatically ($g_{\text{eff}}\to 0$, and the node becomes transparent).

**Honest status of the resonance channel.** In the v1.3/v1.4 implementation, each node's resonance energy is set identically to the node's own energy and the photon energy at that node equals $E_0$ by definition, so the detuning satisfies $\delta\equiv 0$ and the Lorentzian factor in Eq. (1) is identically 1. The resonance channel is *dormant* in the five main variants of Sec. 4.1, where every measured effect is driven by the type-wise contrast of $(g_{\text{couple}}, g_{\text{aether}})$ alone; Sec. 4.1 additionally reports a preliminary activation of $\delta\neq 0$ with a hash-style proxy embedding, ablated and verified lossless (resonant / resonant_hybrid, Table 7), while verification under true semantic embeddings remains future work. We state this explicitly so that no reader overestimates the resonance mechanism's current role.

**Three-channel scattering formulas.** With the normalization constant $\Lambda = 1 + g_{\text{eff}} + g_{\text{aether}}$, the three channel weights are

$$T = \frac{1}{\Lambda},\qquad R = \frac{g_{\text{eff}}}{\Lambda},\qquad A = \frac{g_{\text{aether}}}{\Lambda}, \tag{2}$$

corresponding to transmission, reflection, and reservoir dissipation, respectively. By construction,

$$T + R + A = \frac{1 + g_{\text{eff}} + g_{\text{aether}}}{\Lambda} = 1, \tag{3}$$

which we call *environment-inclusive normalization conservation*: once the dissipation channel is made explicit, the total energy of system-plus-environment is exactly conserved. At the amplitude level one may take $t=\sqrt{T}$, $r=\sqrt{R}$, $a=\sqrt{A}$ with a $\pi/2$ phase convention on the reflected amplitude; every probabilistic statement in this paper depends only on the weights $(T,R,A)$. We emphasize the *boundary of the mapping*: $(T,R,A)$ are classical probability weights satisfying an exact conservation identity; no quantum coherence between paths is modeled or claimed. Implementation requires Eq. (3) to hold within a tolerance of $10^{-6}$ (measured maximum deviation $2.2\times10^{-16}$, Sec. 4.3), and the fully transmitting limit $g_{\text{couple}}=g_{\text{aether}}=0$ ($\Lambda=1$) needs no special branch beyond divide-by-zero protection.

**Feshbach-resonance formal analogy.** The effective form of Eqs. (1)–(2) is *motivated by* a formal analogy with Feshbach's resonance theory of scattering [27, 28]; to be explicit, Eq. (2) is a **constructive definition**, not a corollary of any $S$-matrix theorem, and the normalization is a design choice. Let $S_{\text{bg}}(E)$ be the background scattering matrix (elastic scattering without reservoir coupling), $|W\rangle$ the photon–deposon coupled state (corresponding to a transient bound state of a reasoning path with a trap node), $E_0$ the resonance energy, and $\Gamma_{\text{aether}}$ the reservoir-induced linewidth. The effective $S$-matrix is

$$S_{\text{eff}}(E) = S_{\text{bg}}(E) - \frac{S_{\text{bg}}(E)\,|W\rangle\langle W|\,S_{\text{bg}}(E)}{E - E_0 + i\,\Gamma_{\text{aether}}/2}. \tag{4}$$

The second term of Eq. (4) is the interference correction of an isolated resonance to background scattering: the imaginary part $\Gamma_{\text{aether}}/2$ of the denominator characterizes the decay rate of the bound state into the reservoir continuum — energy thereby acquires a channel that leaves the system degrees of freedom, which is the microscopic origin of the dissipation weight $A$; this follows the experimental tradition of Feshbach resonances in cold-atom physics [29]. Under the single-pole approximation and the Breit–Wigner limit, the resonance cross-section associated with Eq. (4) is proportional to $\frac{1}{\delta^2+(\Gamma/2)^2}$; after normalization this takes the same functional form as the Lorentzian factor of Eq. (1) (details in Appendix A). This formal analogy is what motivates "semantic resonance enhances constraint": the strong reflection/dissipation that a trap node exerts on a matching path is structurally analogous to the peak of a scattering cross-section at the resonance energy. We stress that this is a *formal analogy within scattering theory*, not a claim that reasoning processes are governed by quantum mechanics.

### 3.2 System architecture

![Figure 1: The five-stage Deposon-augmented reasoning pipeline and the three-channel scattering mechanism. (1) Concept decomposition by an LLM backend; (2) deposon field instantiation with semantic binding of scattering parameters; (3) BFS candidate-path generation; (4) three-channel scattering-based screening with per-path energy auditing; (5) answer computation along surviving paths with an independent LLM verification layer.](fig1_architecture.png)

The Deposon-augmented reasoning system consists of a five-stage pipeline (Figure 1):

1. **Concept decomposition (ConceptDecomposer).** An LLM backend (in this paper, the real kimi-for-coding API, with a rule engine as automatic fallback and a persistent disk cache isolated across prompt versions) decomposes a natural-language problem into a concept graph $G$. Node types include *number*, *operation*, *trap* (a decoy candidate that is superficially related but semantically irrelevant), *answer*, and generic *concept* nodes. In the v1.3.1 evaluation, all 199 unique problems were decomposed via the real API, with zero rule-based fallbacks in the final results.

2. **Deposon field instantiation (DeposonField.spawn).** Node types are bound to fixed parameter values (v1.3.1 implementation, verified against the source code): trap nodes receive ($g_{\text{couple}}=5.0$, $g_{\text{aether}}=0.0$) with energy 0.1–0.15 — strong scattering centers; operation nodes receive (0.3, 0.2) with energy 0.4; answer, number, and concept nodes receive (0.05, 0.05); number nodes carry energy $E_{N_i}=0.3+0.05\,i$ by index, and the goal node has energy 0.0. The coupling additionally receives a degree correction $g_{\text{couple}}\times(1+0.02\cdot\text{degree})$. As stated in Sec. 3.1, resonance energies coincide with node energies in this version, so the resonance channel is dormant and the binding acts purely through $(g_{\text{couple}}, g_{\text{aether}})$. The binding is fixed and type-determined rather than learned — this answers the concern that the physical mapping might be decorative (parameters are determined by the node's semantic role, not assigned post hoc), while whether this particular binding is optimal remains an open question (Sec. 5.2).

3. **Candidate path generation (BFS).** Breadth-first search on $G$ generates the set of candidate paths from the start node to answer nodes; v1.3 relaxes the neighbor truncation from 4 to 8 so that decoy trap paths and correct paths are *simultaneously reachable* — a precondition for a valid ablation (see C3 and the root-cause analysis in Sec. 4.1). BFS replaces the Metropolis–Hastings sampling of earlier versions, avoiding the sampling curse of dimensionality in high-dimensional path spaces.

4. **Scattering-based screening (process_path).** For each candidate path, three-channel scattering (Eqs. (1)–(2)) is computed node by node and energy evolves along the path per Eq. (5). The path fate is decided by per-node channel maxima: $\max_i R_i > 0.7 \Rightarrow \text{blocked}$; $\max_i A_i > 0.5 \Rightarrow \text{tunneling}$; otherwise transmitted. The path's final score is its transmitted energy fraction, $\text{score}=E^{(n)}/E^{(0)}$; paths with score above the pass threshold 0.1 are retained, candidates are ranked by score in descending order, and the top-ranked path is selected. The vectorized implementation (NumPy matrix operations) scatters 1,000 deposons in under 0.2 ms, satisfying real-time reasoning constraints.

5. **Answer computation and verification (along surviving paths).** The final answer is computed *strictly along the paths that survive Deposon screening*, rather than bypassing the physical layer and extracting numbers from the whole graph (this decoupling was one root cause of the identically-zero effect size in v1.2; see Sec. 4.1). An independent LLM verification layer judges the correctness of the surviving paths' reasoning chains (trap detection, path completeness, number-usage checks); its over-strict verdicts are honestly recorded in Sec. 4.4 and 5.

### 3.3 Energy flow and irreversibility

**Derivation of energy-flow conservation.** Consider a path of $n$ nodes with incident energy $E^{(0)}$ and channel weights $(T_i,R_i,A_i)$ at node $i$. Transmitted energy evolves recursively along the path:

$$E^{(i)} = E^{(i-1)}\,T_i,\qquad i=1,\dots,n, \tag{5}$$

and the accumulated reflection and dissipation are

$$E_{\text{refl}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} E^{(i-1)} R_i, \qquad E_{\text{diss}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} E^{(i-1)} A_i. \tag{6}$$

**Proposition (per-path energy conservation).** For any path and any node parameters, $E^{(n)} + E_{\text{refl}} + E_{\text{diss}} = E^{(0)}$.

**Proof.** By Eq. (3), $R_i + A_i = 1 - T_i$. Define the partial sum $S_k = E^{(k)} + \sum_{i=1}^{k} E^{(i-1)}(R_i+A_i)$. Then

$$S_k = E^{(k-1)}T_k + E^{(k-1)}(1-T_k) + \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} E^{(i-1)}(R_i+A_i) = S_{k-1},$$

and induction down to $S_0 = E^{(0)}$ completes the proof. $\square$

This conservation law renders the energy flow of every reasoning path fully auditable: each unit of energy either reaches the endpoint, is reflected, or is deposited into the reservoir — exactly one of the three (the audit implementation is described in Sec. 4.3).

**Irreversibility of the EtherChannel.** The reservoir channel is one-way at the interface level: `dissipate(energy)` atomically accumulates energy into a monotone non-decreasing counter, and the system *provides no* `recover()` method; once deposited, the entry is locked (`is_locked=True`). This engineering constraint corresponds to the following physical argument.

**Argument (failure of Poincaré recurrence).** The Poincaré recurrence theorem presupposes a finite invariant measure (finite phase-space volume): under this premise, almost every orbit returns arbitrarily close to its initial state, with a typical recurrence time growing exponentially in the number of degrees of freedom. The reservoir is modeled as an *infinite-dimensional orthogonal environment* (`capacity = ∞`): its state-space dimension is unbounded, the invariant measure is no longer finite, and the theorem's premise fails; once deposited energy disperses into infinitely many orthogonal modes, the recurrence time diverges and dissipation is irreversible in the asymptotic sense. Two honest disclaimers. (i) In any finite-dimensional software realization, "irreversibility" is a combination of engineering enforcement (locking plus a monotone counter) and an asymptotic property (the infinite-dimensional limit), not a mathematical theorem under any finite truncation — the infinite-dimensional approximation is an irreducible theoretical limitation (Sec. 5). (ii) It is precisely this irreversibility that distinguishes Deposon from probabilistic pruning: the energy budget of an eliminated path cannot flow back, so errors cannot "revive" to occupy subsequent search resources.

### 3.4 Five-variant ablation design

To isolate the independent contribution of each mechanism, we run five variants on the *same* concept graphs and the *same* candidate path sets (Table 1). All variants except no_deposon share the scattering engine and path generator; the only difference is the deposon parameter configuration, which keeps the ablation controlled.

**Table 1. Five-variant ablation configuration.**

| Variant | $g_{\text{couple}}$ | $g_{\text{aether}}$ | Mode | Test objective |
|---------|--------------------|--------------------|------|----------------|
| no_deposon | — (no deposon field) | — | greedy routing baseline | performance ceiling of concept graph + BFS alone |
| v1_blocking | high (strong coupling on trap nodes) | 0 | BLOCKING | whether pure reflection eliminates traps |
| v2_tunneling | low | high | TUNNELING | whether pure dissipation sacrifices accuracy |
| unified | medium | medium | GENERAL | whether the two mechanisms cooperate optimally |
| high_couple | highest (all $g_{\text{couple}}\times 3$, post-fix) | 0 | BLOCKING (extreme) | whether constraint strength saturates / overshoots |

Each variant runs on all 100 items of both benchmarks (seed=42; the evaluation is deterministic end-to-end). Metrics include accuracy, mean reservoir dissipation, stratified accuracy by operation type and by trap type, three-channel energy allocation, and unitarity deviation. The McNemar significance-testing design against GSM8K baselines is given in Sec. 4.2.

### 4.2 Controlled comparison on a real GSM8K subset ($n=100$, seed=42) against CoT baselines

The experiment for this subsection is complete (final version, $n=100$, seed=42); the design and results follow.

**Design.** We draw 100 problems from the GSM8K test set [11] with seed=42 to form the real subset. Three pipelines are compared: (i) **CoT baseline** — the same LLM backend answers directly with chain-of-thought prompting (one zero-shot CoT variant [2] and one few-shot CoT variant [1]); (ii) **no_deposon** — concept decomposition plus BFS greedy routing without the deposon field; (iii) **Deposon-unified** — the full Sec. 3.2 pipeline. All three pipelines share the same LLM backend and cache layer to isolate the causal contribution of the deposon layer. Answers are judged by exact match (final-number extraction with floating-point equality tolerance). Statistical testing uses McNemar's test [34]: from the discordant pairs $(b,c)$ of Deposon-unified versus the CoT baseline (numbers of problems solved by exactly one of the two), the statistic $\chi^2 = (|b-c|-1)^2/(b+c)$ is constructed at significance level $\alpha=0.05$; we additionally report Wilson 95% confidence intervals and the effect size (accuracy difference). The primary endpoint is overall accuracy; the secondary endpoint is accuracy stratified by the number of reasoning steps (testing the prediction that the physical layer's increment is larger on longer chains, Sec. 5.1).

**Interpretation framework (pre-committed, before seeing the numbers).** This subsection pre-commits to a bidirectional reading: the primary question is not "does Deposon beat CoT" but "what is the true cost of the constraint layer." (i) If unified ≥ CoT, scattering constraints are approximately lossless on the real benchmark and the synthetic-set anti-capture gains come nearly for free. (ii) If unified < CoT, the gap quantifies the information-loss cost of concept decomposition, and the layer's value proposition must be restated as "trading a quantifiable accuracy cost for auditability and anti-capture protection." Under either outcome, the McNemar discordant pairs and the failure attribution locate the cost in a specific mechanism.

**Primary-protocol results (pre-committed protocol).** Table 10 gives the final results ($n=100$, seed=42, decompose prompt v1.3.2; 600 real API calls, 0 fallbacks): CoT baseline **97.0%**, unified 85.0%, v1_blocking 86.0%, high_couple 86.0%, v2_tunneling 4.0%, no_deposon 2.0%. McNemar tests: unified vs CoT has discordant pairs $b=0,\ c=12$, $p=4.9\times10^{-4}$ — **CoT is significantly better; we report this negative conclusion honestly**; unified vs no_deposon gives $b=84,\ c=1$, $p=4.4\times10^{-24}$. The independent verification layer (validate, 100 items, all real API calls) agrees with the gold standard at 97%; the maximum unitarity-audit deviation is $2.2\times10^{-16}$. The outcome is thereby settled as case (ii) of the framework: on real, clean inputs the constraint layer inherits and amplifies the upstream decomposition/folding bottleneck, paying a quantifiable cost of 12 percentage points relative to direct CoT.

## 5 Limitations and Ethics

### 5.1 The boundary of Deposon's incremental value

We interpret the effect sizes of Sec. 4.1 with restraint: **they measure the increment of path selection *within a fixed concept graph*, not any improvement of the LLM's intrinsic reasoning capability.** In the synthetic benchmarks, concept decomposition is performed by the same LLM backend, and the knowledge required for the correct operations is already present in the graph; the greedy baseline's 7%/10% accuracy reflects the systematic failure of greedy routing when decoys are reachable, and Deposon's contribution is to select the correct path within the graph. In other words, Sec. 4.1 demonstrates that "*when knowledge is already available, a physical constraint layer can solve the path-selection problem nearly perfectly*" — not that "Deposon teaches the LLM problems it could not otherwise solve." The latter can only be answered by the GSM8K+CoT comparison of Sec. 4.2, which is precisely why we designate it the primary pending experiment. Accordingly, we make no claims of state-of-the-art performance, and we do not assert superiority over LLM reasoning in general.

This yields a characterization of where Deposon applies. (i) *Trap-dense / long-chain tasks* — when candidate paths are numerous, decoys are reachable, and single-chain errors are costly, irreversible elimination by the physical layer is most valuable (the trap set of Sec. 4.1 is a controlled limit of this kind). (ii) *Simple short-chain tasks* — when baseline routing is already reliable, Deposon does no harm but also adds nothing: on easy items we observed no gain attributable to the physical layer (the v1.2 simple-set plateau of 85% taught us that the constraint layer's ceiling is set by decomposition quality), and we expect no super-baseline gains on the easy portion of the GSM8K subset either; the step-stratified secondary endpoint of Sec. 4.2 is designed to test exactly this prediction. (iii) *Knowledge-missing tasks* — if the concept graph lacks the nodes a correct path requires, no screening mechanism can help; the bottleneck is upstream decomposition, not the physical layer. We predict that on GSM8K, Deposon's increment grows with the number of reasoning steps.

**The constraint–fidelity trade-off.** Combining the synthetic-benchmark results with the final GSM8K results (Sec. 4.2), Deposon's value proposition should not be stated as "beating CoT across the board" but delimited as "**auditable protection in decoy-dense environments**": when candidate paths contain adversarial decoys (the synthetic trap set), the constraint layer provides protection at a +0.90 effect size with per-step auditability; when inputs are clean but upstream decomposition incurs information loss (real multi-step English problems), the constraint layer inherits — and may amplify — the decomposition bottleneck, exhibiting a quantifiable accuracy cost relative to direct CoT. Constraint strength and input fidelity thus form a characterizable trade-off: the harder the protection layer, the higher its sensitivity to upstream graph quality — the labelfree ablation (Sec. 4.1, Table 8) is precisely an attempt to shift this sensitivity from type labels to edge-weight signatures. This two-sidedness — anti-capture gains versus information-loss costs — constitutes the complete scientific profile of an "auditable constraint layer," which we consider more valuable for research than one-sided good news.

### 5.2 Limitations

We list limitations in three classes — irreducible, mitigable, and must-declare.

**Theoretical limitations (irreducible).** (1) *Finite-dimensional approximation of an infinite-dimensional reservoir*: any numerical realization must truncate the theoretically infinite-dimensional environment; "irreversibility" is a combination of an asymptotic property and engineering locking, not a mathematical theorem under any finite truncation (Sec. 3.3). (2) *No general theory of parameters*: the optimal $(g_{\text{couple}}, g_{\text{aether}})$ is task-dependent, with no closed-form mapping from semantics to parameters; the node-type binding used here is heuristic. (3) *Unverified energy metaphor*: the correspondence between "cognitive energy" and physical energy is a heuristic analogy whose deeper justification awaits theoretical work.

**Engineering and evaluation limitations (mitigable).** (4) *Scale*: all benchmark evidence is at the 100-item scale per dataset (200 items total); while evaluation is deterministic and effect sizes are large, conclusions at this scale do not automatically transfer to thousand-item regimes. (5) *Single model family*: all decomposition and verification used one LLM backend (kimi-for-coding); cross-model-family robustness is untested, and results may not transfer to other LLMs. (6) *Decomposition quality ceiling*: system accuracy is bounded above by the concept decomposer; v1.3 upgraded from a rule engine to a real LLM backend, but decomposition of complex semantics (e.g., nested multi-step operations) can still fail. (7) *Over-strict verification layer*: the independent LLM verifier agrees with the gold standard at only 0.86 (simple) / 0.78 (trap), with systematic false positives on node-path-style reasoning chains (Sec. 4.4); we do not feed verification results back into the main loop and retain the layer as an audit signal only. (8) *Quadratic conjugate identification*: conjugate-pair identification costs $O(|V|^2 d)$, requiring approximate acceleration (e.g., LSH) on large graphs; the resonance channel has been activated losslessly by the resonant/resonant_hybrid ablations (Sec. 4.1, Table 7), but the current $E_{\text{photon}}$ uses a hash-style deterministic embedding (no semantics, with a self-similarity bias) and its behavior under true semantic embeddings remains to be verified. (9) *Residual form of the label dependence*: the labelfree ablation (Table 8) shows that the type-label dependence can be removed *at scoring time* (25/25 bit-identical under label shuffling); however, the structural signals it exploits are themselves transcribed from type labels (label→weight), so genuine label-information independence awaits edge weights from an independent source (LLM continuous confidences or learned weights) — and if a graph constructor flattens decoy and correct edge weights, the signal fails. The perfect separation was obtained only on synthetic constructed graphs. (10) *Distribution shift of the synthetic benchmarks*: the distribution of our self-built trap benchmark is deliberately engineered (including adversarially weighted decoy edges, see the root-cause analysis in Sec. 4.1), and extrapolating its effect sizes to natural distributions requires caution — which is exactly the motivation for Sec. 4.2.

**Boundaries of the ensemble and barrier extensions (honestly reported negative results).** (a) *Time-for-quality (G2)*: on the two already-saturated benchmarks, neither Boltzmann annealing nor path-integral ensembling yields any accuracy gain, at a 4.2–4.5× energy cost (Sec. 4.5, Table 9) — trading time for quality may pay off only on unsaturated or harder tasks; this hypothesis remains to be verified. (b) *Arrhenius barrier channel (G3)*: we activated the 33 dormant migration_barrier edges with a per-edge multiplicative decay $k=e^{-b/T_{\text{eff}}}$ (energy accounting $t+r+a+\text{barrier\_loss}=1$ verified path by path). At $T\ge0.5$ both modes match unified on both benchmarks (100%) — the barrier channel is harmless in the high-temperature limit. But at $T\le0.3$ trap-set accuracy drops to 84%: all failures concentrate on two-step problems (percentage_subtraction) whose correct chain has one more edge than the decoy path — a **path-length bias**: at low temperature the correct chain pays one extra barrier-crossing tax. The Kramers correction ($k=\min(1,\,w\,e^{-b/T_{\text{eff}}})$) changes no accuracy. The deeper structural reason is a **barrier–semantics mismatch**: in the current construction the decoy edges are *low*-barrier (0.1, as part of the decoy design), so "low barrier = easy migration" favors the decoys — the channel would only be useful under a graph construction in which barrier height correlates positively with semantic risk. Honest disclosure: barrier values are hand-set at construction time (0.2–0.3 for correct edges), not learned.

**Must declare.** (11) There is no real Deposon chip; the hardware mapping of Sec. 5.4 is a conceptual design, and all "hardware" statements are software simulations. (12) Long-term stability (across prompt versions and model versions) is only preliminarily guarded by cache-version isolation; no year-scale tracking exists.

### 5.3 Ethics statement

This work uses only self-constructed synthetic benchmarks and public datasets (GSM8K [11]); no human subjects, personal data, or crowdworkers are involved. The computational footprint is modest (approximately 370,000 API tokens for the full evaluation campaign). We see no foreseeable dual-use risk: the method constrains reasoning-path selection and does not amplify generative capabilities. Our main ethical commitment is *honest reporting*: all negative results (verifier false positives, the absence of gains on easy items, and the v1.2 zero-effect episode) are disclosed rather than omitted, and all claimed numbers are reproducible from the released evaluation JSON artifacts.

### 5.4 Hardware-isomorphism outlook

The three-parameter family of Deposon admits an explicit bidirectional mapping to semiconductor/photonic devices, reserving an interface for an algorithm-to-chip path:

$$g_{\text{couple}} = -\ln(1-p) \;\Leftrightarrow\; p = 1 - e^{-g_{\text{couple}}} \quad(\text{PCM crystalline fraction});$$

$$\eta = \frac{g_{\text{aether}}}{1+g_{\text{aether}}} \;\Leftrightarrow\; g_{\text{aether}} = \frac{\eta}{1-\eta} \quad(\text{MZI splitting ratio});$$

$$\Gamma_{\text{aether}} = \gamma_{\text{ECM}}\cdot\psi(t) \quad(\text{ECM ion migration}),$$

where $p$ is the crystalline fraction of a phase-change memory (PCM) cell (SET pulses crystallize and increase $p$; RESET pulses amorphize and decrease $p$), $\eta$ is the Mach–Zehnder interferometer splitting ratio ($\eta=0$, full reflection, corresponds to v1; $\eta=1$, full transmission into the reservoir port, to v2), and $\gamma_{\text{ECM}}$ and $\psi(t)$ are the ion mobility and temporal modulation of an electrochemical memory (ECM) cell. Physically, this means "reasoning-constraint strength" could be encoded directly as device state; neuromorphic photonics [25] has demonstrated the engineering feasibility of this class of mappings. We claim no hardware results in this paper; tape-out verification and software–hardware consistency testing belong to the v2.0 roadmap.

## 6 Conclusion

We presented Deposon, a physics-constrained scattering layer for auditable LLM reasoning path selection, together with its unified-limits formulation: nodes of an LLM-generated concept graph are bound to deposon states, and candidate reasoning paths are screened by Feshbach-resonance-style three-channel scattering (transmission / reflection / irreversible dissipation, $T+R+A=1$), under which blocking (v1) and tunneling (v2) emerge as two limit regimes of a single entity in reservoir-coupling strength. On controlled synthetic benchmarks (100 simple + 100 trap items; seed=42; a real LLM backend with zero fallbacks), the unified variant raises accuracy from the greedy baseline's 7%/10% to 100% (effect sizes +0.93/+0.90), covering all six categories of the trap taxonomy; the physical audit confirms a machine-precision unitarity deviation of $2.2\times10^{-16}$, limit-regime dissipation rates of 0%/86.92%/8.63% matching the theoretical predictions, and irreversible reservoir deposition. We also contribute a root-cause analysis of two ablation failure modes (answer–screening decoupling; unreachable traps), providing a design paradigm for controlled ablation in physics-constraint-layer research.

**Limitations.** The current effect sizes measure only the within-graph path-selection increment, not an improvement over the LLM itself (Sec. 5.1); evidence is at the 100-item scale on a single model family; the verification layer is over-strict and produces false positives; the infinite-dimensional reservoir is realizable only as a finite-dimensional approximation; and the optimal scattering parameters lack a general theory.

**Future work.** Near term (v1.4–v2.0): complete the real GSM8K-subset comparison against CoT baselines with McNemar testing (Sec. 4.2); activate and evaluate node-conjugate mapping; replace hand-tied parameter binding with learned, load-adaptive adjustment of $(g_{\text{couple}}, g_{\text{aether}})$. Long term: tape-out verification of the PCM/MZI/ECM hardware mapping, and transfer to trap-dense/long-chain tasks such as StrategyQA [12] and long-document understanding.

### A. Derivation of the scattering equations

This appendix presents the *formal analogy* that motivates the normalized three-channel weights (Eq. (2)): the chain runs from the Feshbach effective $S$-matrix (Eq. (4)) as a source of intuition, while Eq. (2) itself is a constructive definition and its normalization a design choice — not a corollary of the $S$-matrix formalism.

**A.1 Single-pole approximation.** Partition the total Hilbert space into an open-channel subspace $P$ (the continuum modes of reasoning paths) and an isolated-resonance subspace $Q$ (the photon–deposon bound state $|W\rangle$). Feshbach projection yields the effective $S$-matrix on $P$ [27, 28]:

$$S_{\text{eff}}(E) = S_{\text{bg}}(E)\left[\mathbb{1} - \frac{|W\rangle\langle W|}{E - E_0 + i\Gamma_{\text{aether}}/2}\right],$$

where $\Gamma_{\text{aether}}$ is the linewidth induced by the decay of the $Q$-subspace into the reservoir continuum (coupling of $Q$ to the infinite-dimensional reservoir endows the isolated level with the imaginary part $-i\Gamma_{\text{aether}}/2$).

**A.2 Breit–Wigner limit and the Lorentzian lineshape.** In the single-channel, single-resonance limit, the resonance contribution to the scattering cross-section is proportional to

$$\sigma_{\text{res}}(E) \propto \frac{(\Gamma/2)^2}{(E-E_0)^2 + (\Gamma/2)^2}.$$

Taking the linewidth as the natural scale unit ($\Gamma/2 = 1$) and writing the detuning as $\delta = |E - E_0|$ yields the resonance factor $\frac{1}{1+\delta^2}$, i.e., the Lorentzian modulating $g_{\text{couple}}\to g_{\text{eff}}$ in Eq. (1). Physical reading: the better the path energy matches the node's resonance energy, the stronger the bound-state formation and the stronger the scattering (reflection/dissipation); far off resonance, the interaction closes adiabatically.

**A.3 Three-channel normalization.** At fixed $\delta$, the incident flux faces three mutually exclusive and exhaustive exits: (i) interaction-free transmission (reference weight 1); (ii) reflection via the effective coupling $g_{\text{eff}}$; (iii) dissipation via the reservoir coupling $g_{\text{aether}}$. Normalizing by $\Lambda = 1 + g_{\text{eff}} + g_{\text{aether}}$ gives Eq. (2). The identity $T+R+A=1$ (Eq. (3)) holds by construction; it expresses, at the observable level, the joint unitarity of "system + reservoir": dissipation does not violate unitarity but reallocates it once the environment is included. The machine-precision audit of Sec. 4.3 (maximum deviation $2.2\times10^{-16}$) verifies the identity's strictness in floating-point implementation. Scope note: this identity is a classical probability-conservation statement in our algorithm; the scattering-theoretic language is a formal analogy and implies no quantum-mechanical claim about the reasoning process.

**A.4 Limit-regime checks.** The behavior of Eq. (2) at three boundaries: (i) $g_{\text{aether}}=0$: $A=0$ and $T+R=1$ — the pure v1 closed system; (ii) $g_{\text{aether}}\to\infty$: $A\to 1$ and $T,R\to 0$; note, however, that the operating regime of v2 tunneling is $g_{\text{aether}}\gg g_{\text{eff}}$ with small $g_{\text{eff}}$ — then $T\approx 1$ for correct (low-coupling) nodes while energy at trap nodes (high $g_{\text{eff}}$) is largely absorbed by $A$, which realizes "lossless transmission of the correct path with deposition of erroneous energy"; (iii) $g_{\text{couple}}=g_{\text{aether}}=0$: $\Lambda=1$ and $T=1$ — full transmission (the transparent limit corresponding to no_deposon). The three limits connect continuously in parameter space; this is the mathematical content of the unified-limits proposition.

### B. Benchmark construction details and the trap taxonomy

**B.1 Construction principles.** Both benchmarks contain 100 items each and are generated deterministically with seed=42. Each item is produced together with: the problem text, the gold operation type, the gold answer, a trap-category label, and (from v1.3 onward) a *decoy trap path* in the concept graph — a superficially plausible but semantically wrong path, ensured to be reachable (BFS neighbor truncation 4→8). The decoy-reachability design is a precondition of ablation validity: if the baseline BFS cannot reach the trap at all, "Deposon blocks the trap" is untestable (root cause two of the identically-zero effect size in v1.2).

**B.2 The six-category trap taxonomy.** (i) none: no decoy (control group); (ii) surface_addition: the statement contains addition-triggering words ("altogether," "in total") but the gold operation is not addition; (iii) surface_subtraction: subtraction-context words ("gave away," "left") whose operative relation disagrees with the surface cue; (iv) surface_multiplication / surface_division: trigger words ("times," "split evenly") but a different gold operation; (v) wrong_order: multi-step items in which the surface reading order reverses the correct operation order (e.g., "a discount applied before a threshold reduction").
