## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) remain vulnerable to surface-association traps in multi-step reasoning: when a problem statement contains numerical cues that are superficially related but semantically irrelevant to the correct solution, greedy path selection is systematically deflected toward decoy branches. We present Deposon, a physics-constrained layer that maps an LLM-generated concept graph onto a collection of node-bound scattering centers and subjects candidate reasoning paths to a three-channel scattering process — transmission, reflection, and irreversible dissipation into a formal "ether" reservoir (a purely terminological borrowing; no physical-ontology claim is made). The two behaviors of earlier designs, blocking (v1) and tunneling (v2), are shown to be two limit regimes of a single two-parameter family; the scattering weights are a constructive definition (satisfying the exact identity $T+R+A=1$ for all parameter values) motivated by a formal analogy with Feshbach-resonance-style effective $S$-matrices. On two controlled synthetic benchmarks (100 simple arithmetic items and 100 surface-association trap items; seed=42; concept graphs produced by a real LLM backend with zero rule-based fallbacks in the final run), the unified variant raises accuracy from 7% (simple) and 10% (trap) for the greedy no-Deposon baseline to 100% on both sets — within-graph selection effect sizes of +0.93 and +0.90 — and reaches 100% on all six categories of our trap taxonomy. A system-wide physical audit reports a maximum unitarity deviation of $2.2\times10^{-16}$ (machine precision), and limit-regime dissipation rates (0% / 86.92% / 8.63%) that match the theoretical predictions. We additionally specify a fixed protocol for a controlled comparison against chain-of-thought baselines on a real GSM8K subset. Final results (primary protocol, pre-committed before the experiment): on the real GSM8K subset, CoT reaches 97.0% and Deposon-unified 85.0% (McNemar $p=4.9\times10^{-4}$, CoT significantly better) — the bidirectional interpretation framework pre-committed in Sec. 4.2 is resolved in its "information-loss cost dominates" outcome, and we report this without embellishment. We emphasize throughout that the reported effect sizes measure the *increment of path selection within a fixed concept graph*, not an improvement of the LLM's intrinsic reasoning capability. Two further controls sharpen this boundary: a label-shuffle ablation (5 seeds) collapses trap-benchmark accuracy from 100% to 17.2%±6.4%, and a uniform-params control to 10% — the layer is a *transducer* of semantic labels into auditable energy decisions, not an independent discriminator. We also disclose that the no_deposon baseline is decoy-baited by graph construction (decoy edges weighted 0.9 vs 0.6); against the neutral uniform-params control (which coincides numerically with the greedy baseline on both sets) the increments are +0.93 (simple) and +0.90 (trap). Two further algorithmic ablations sharpen the picture: the label dependence can be removed *at scoring time* (labelfree computes the binding from structural signals without reading type strings — 100% on both benchmarks, 25/25 bit-identical under label shuffling); however, in the current pipeline these structural signals are themselves transcribed from type labels (label→weight), so genuine label-information independence remains to be verified with weights from an independent source — the residual dependence is honestly disclosed; and the dormant resonance channel can be activated losslessly (resonant/resonant_hybrid both 100%). Finally, we pre-commit to a bidirectional interpretation framework for the real-benchmark comparison (Sec. 4.2): whether the constraint layer exhibits anti-capture gains or information-loss costs on real tasks, both outcomes fall within the same framework — anti-capture gains and information-loss costs together form the complete scientific profile of an auditable constraint layer. This boundary, together with an honest account of verifier false positives, is treated as a first-class part of the contribution.

**Keywords:** physics-inspired reasoning; energy dissipation; Feshbach resonance; large language models; reasoning path selection; irreversibility; auditability

## 1 Introduction

Large language models have achieved striking progress on multi-step reasoning tasks such as math word problems, commonsense inference, and code generation [1, 2, 11]. Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting [1] elicits intermediate reasoning steps and substantially improves arithmetic and symbolic reasoning; Tree of Thoughts (ToT) [3] and Self-Consistency (SC) [5] generalize single-chain reasoning into search and voting, respectively. Taken together, these results suggest that the reasoning quality of an LLM depends not only on model scale but, critically, on **how its intermediate reasoning paths are organized**.

Multi-step reasoning reliability is nevertheless limited by three structural problems. First, *surface-association traps*: numbers and keywords in the problem text that are superficially related but semantically irrelevant to the correct solution (e.g., the word "altogether" suggesting addition when the correct operation is subtraction) systematically attract LLMs and their search-based derivatives toward decoy branches [36, 37]. Second, *error compounding along chains*: in CoT/ToT-style methods, an erroneous intermediate step carries no intrinsic cost, so descendants of an error node continue to consume the search budget. Third, *uninterpretability of path selection*: voting and heuristic scoring cannot answer the structural question of *why* a given path should be eliminated, and therefore cannot be audited.

More fundamentally, existing methods eliminate wrong paths *reversibly*: a pruned branch can be revived at the next expansion, the next sample, or the next prompt. This stands in contrast to the physical world — in open quantum systems, once energy dissipates into an environment in the thermodynamic-limit idealization of infinitely many degrees of freedom, the Poincaré recurrence time diverges and dissipation is irreversible for all practical purposes [30, 31]. Our position is that such intrinsic properties of physical systems — irreversible dissipation, resonance enhancement, and unitarity constraints — are not decorative metaphors but structural constraints that can be built directly into an algorithm's cost and conservation structure, essentially for free. Throughout this paper, every physical correspondence is stated as a **formal mapping with an explicit mathematical boundary**; we make no claim that the algorithm instantiates any real physical process.

We formalize this intuition as **Deposon**, named after *deposition*: the phase transition in which energy passes directly from a gaseous to a solid phase, dispersing irreversibly into an (idealized) infinite-dimensional orthogonal reservoir. Concretely, an LLM first decomposes a problem into a concept graph whose nodes are numbers, operations, traps, and answer candidates; each node is bound to a *deposon state* characterized by two parameters — a coupling strength $g_{\text{couple}}$ to reasoning paths and a coupling strength $g_{\text{aether}}$ to the reservoir. A reasoning path is treated as a stream of "photon" amplitude traversing the deposon field, undergoing three-channel scattering at each node: transmission ($T$), reflection ($R$), and irreversible dissipation into the reservoir ($A$), with the strict identity $T+R+A=1$. Blocking (v1: $g_{\text{aether}}=0$, wrong paths are locally reflected and attenuated) and tunneling (v2: $g_{\text{aether}}\gg0$, erroneous energy is deposited into the reservoir while the correct path transmits nearly losslessly) thereby emerge as two limit regimes of a single entity rather than two separate mechanisms.

We evaluate five ablation variants on two controlled benchmarks of 100 items each (seed=42), with concept graphs produced by a real LLM backend and zero rule-based fallbacks in the final run. As shown in Tables 2 and 3, the unified variant reaches 100% accuracy on both the simple and trap benchmarks, whereas the greedy no-Deposon baseline on the *same* concept graphs reaches only 7% and 10%. The physical audit shows a maximum unitarity deviation of $2.2\times10^{-16}$ across all paths, and the limit-regime energy allocations (v1 dissipation 0%, v2 dissipation 86.92%, unified dissipation 8.63%) match the theoretical predictions.
