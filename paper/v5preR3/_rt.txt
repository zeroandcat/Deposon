Abstract
The deposon (Chinese ningzi) is a physics-analogue scattering-dissipation theory of cognitive
energy in reasoning systems. Its ontology layer is a two-parameter three-channel scattering law: at
every node the incident cognitive energy is split into a transmitted, a reﬂected and a dissipated share
with |t|2 + |r|2 + |a|2 = 1; the split is modulated pointwise by a coupling strength gcouple and an
aether openness gaether ; the dissipated share goes into an inﬁnite-dimensional orthogonal complement of the real cognitive space and, once there, does not return, because the generating Lindblad
semigroup has no completely positive trace-preserving inverse and no general recurrence guarantee is available in inﬁnite dimension. Ledgers, channel notation, tombstones and phantom rows are
the tool-representation layer of that law, not the theory. “Physics-analogue” means a constructive
mapping—a system is built to obey the prescribed structure and is veriﬁed to obey it—and not the
approximate validity of physical law; photon, dark matter, hole, aether and heat bath are metaphorlayer words and are cited to their source segments, never used as evidence. The falsiﬁcation condition
of the theory is the existence of an inverse process that recovers dissolved content; this paper gives
that condition an operational criterion, states what a readback is in the theory (a new scattering event
on the reality-space side), and speciﬁes the executor layer without which no deployed system counts
as a complete deposon. The per-path audit of the deployed prototype closes the conservation identity
to 2.2 × 10−16 ; the behavioural readings inherited from the tool-face paper (indistinguishable from a
six-keyword rule ﬁlter; convex-combination dilution) are retained verbatim as a negative boundary,
attributed to working in the dark-matter picture under a misplaced parameter prior rather than to the
law; the lost-key prediction has zero readings on record and is stated as untested. A pre-registered
three-arm design, the candidate metrics and the positive-region hypothesis for the free-parameter
space are speciﬁed as the test.

The deposon: deﬁnition and etymology

1.1 Deﬁnition
Core claim. Congeal-by-deposition: vapour condenses to solid without passing through liquid; energy
is deposited into an irreversible sink and never ﬂows back.
The deposon (Chinese ningzi, the congeal particle; English Deposition + −on) names the quasiparticle of cognitive energy: the unit in which an active reasoning state is deposited into a dormant state
and, in being deposited, releases its energy into a sink from which it does not return (T-CONGELON004, naming and physical deﬁnition). The name is the ﬁrst statement of the theory. The ﬁrst character
of the name, ning (to congeal), is the character of ninghua (deposition), not of ninggu (freezing), and the
distinction carries the whole ontology. Freezing is a liquid-to-solid transition that is reversed by heating:
the energy remains in the system. Deposition is a gas-to-solid transition that releases latent heat into
the environment; in the sink this paper posits—inﬁnite-dimensional, orthogonal to the system, without a
ﬁnite measure-preserving phase volume (§2.3)—that latent heat is not recoverable. Irreversibility is the

from the cognitive system, preventing accumulation and overheating. The sufﬁx -on places the object in
the series of particle names (electron, photon, phonon) and claims for it only what a quasi-particle claim
is: a bookkeeping unit with a deﬁned coupling, not a microscopic body.
In this paper the deposon is a physics-analogue scattering-dissipation theory of cognitive energy in
reasoning systems. Its ontology layer is a two-parameter three-channel scattering law (T + R + A = 1,
irreversible dissipation, append-only monotone). Ledgers, channel notation, tombstones and phantom
rows are its tool-representation layer. The same entity carries the two names: deposon and ningzi are one
concept, not two, and the lowercase common-noun usage of the voided version is replaced here by the
theory’s name [1].

1.2 Category discipline: constructive mapping, not approximation
“Physics-analogue” means a constructive mapping: a system is built and prescribed to obey a selected
scattering-dissipation structure, and the construction is veriﬁed to obey it. It does not mean that physical
laws hold approximately for reasoning systems, and nothing in this paper is a measurement of a physical
quantity. Three layers of words are kept apart and marked where they appear. The axiom layer is the
T + R + A allocation law and its two parameters (§2). The metaphor layer is photon, dark matter, hole,
aether, Feshbach resonance, deposition (ninghua) and heat bath; these supply vocabulary and intuition,
are cited to their source segments, and are never used as ontological evidence. The tool-representation
layer is the ledger, the channel notation, tombstones, phantom rows and the executor (§2.7, §4, §5). A
sentence may move between layers only by saying so. The previous version of this paper (v5preR2)
treated the ledger and the three channels as the core and was voided for exactly this category mismatch;
its data-level assets (bibliography, readings, compilation and freeze tool chain) are inherited without
deletion, and its narrative is demoted to an audit-tool subsection (§7.4).

1.4 Positioning against neighbouring programmes
The deposon shares vocabulary with four neighbouring programmes and differs from each in what it
claims. (i) External memory for agents places state outside the model [2–4]; the deposon agrees on
the placement and asks a different question—not what the layer stores but whether it can dissipate. (ii)
Machine unlearning for agents removes or suppresses knowledge in weights and in the agent’s memory channels [5–7]; its object is content and its success criterion is that the content can no longer be
extracted. The deposon’s object is different in kind: it does not delete a state, it books the dissipation
of that state’s energy and makes the loss auditable (§2.7); a deleted state and a dissipated state are both
absent, and only the latter leaves a tombstone. The two are therefore not rival answers to one question:
unlearning asks whether the content is gone, the deposon asks whether the loss is booked and honest.
(iii) Stochastic thermodynamics of memory, forgetting and learning gives the accounting vocabulary—
exergy, dissipation, cost of prediction and of memory [8–18], and, as a neighbouring use of dissipation,
dissipation-driven selection in driven self-assembly [19]; the deposon takes from it the discipline of exact bookkeeping and not a claim about physical energy, and the exact relation is stated by the absorbed
reconstruction (§2.8). (iv) Auditable agent memory designs ledgers so that a reader can recompute what
happened [20–25], with provenance tracking for machine-learning pipelines [26] as the ancestor of that
kind of ledger; the ledger of §2.7 is in that family and is an instrument, not the theory. Field-theoretic
memory for agents [27] shares the word “ﬁeld”; here the word is metaphor layer (§1.2).

1.5 Roadmap
§2 states the law and its formal status. §3 shows that the two inherited pictures—the hole picture and the
dark-matter picture—are limits of the one law. §4 speciﬁes the executor layer without which the law is
stated but not realised, and gives the falsiﬁcation condition its operational form. §5 states what the law
buys on the memory side, and what it forbids. §6 reports what has been measured, what has not, and the
pre-registered shape of the test. §7 ﬁxes the negative boundary and the category clariﬁcations that follow
from it.

2.2 The two parameters and their pointwise modulation
The scattering amplitudes are governed by two parameters (M1 ğğ2.2–2.3): the coupling strength gcouple ,
the source’s “photon–deposon coupling strength (the blocking strength of v1)”, and the aether openness
gaether , the source’s “aether openness (the energy dissipation channel of v2)”; both quoted phrases are
translations of M1 ğ2.3, and the dimensionless coupling constant of M1 ğ2.2 is the same openness in
ratio form. Two properties matter for everything that follows.
Pointwise, not global. Each node carries its own pair (gcouple , gaether )i ; the parameters are attributes
of the node’s state and may be set per node and per task, not universal constants of the system. A
reasoning graph is therefore a ﬁeld of operating points, and a statement about “the” parameters of a run
is a statement about a prior placed on that ﬁeld (§7.2).
Ratio, not magnitude. The regime is determined by the ratio η = gaether /gcouple : η ≪ 1 is the
blocking-dominated limit, η ≫ 1 the tunnelling-dominated limit, η ∼ 1 a mixed state (M1 ğ6.1). The
two limits are the subject of §3. In the Lindblad generator of §2.4 the rate γaether is set by gaether , and in
the resonance form of §2.6 the width Γaether grows with gaether ; the paper keeps g for the two parameters
and γ, Γ for the rates they induce, and does not conﬂate them.

Three facts ﬁx the formal status of irreversibility, and all three are standard mathematics rather than
properties of the analogy. (i) The semigroup generated by (5) is completely positive and trace preserving (CPTP); in ﬁnite dimension every such uniform semigroup is of Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan–
Lindblad form [28–30]. (ii) A non-unitary CPTP semigroup has no CPTP inverse: the invertible quantum
operations are the unitaries together with a ﬁxed ancilla (Theorem 2.1 of Nayak and Sen) [31]; that the
quasi-inverse used in practice is a ﬁdelity-optimising surrogate and not an inverse is a supporting observation [32], cited as corroboration and not for the invertibility theorem. (iii) In ﬁnite dimension a
Lindblad evolution reaches a steady state and the quantum regression theorem allows partial recovery of
correlations; the source’s statement that this fails for the inﬁnite-dimensional aether is the premise P2,
which the next subsection separates from any claim about a physical system. What the theory forbids
is therefore not “an inverse we have not built” but “an inverse of the generator”: a device that recovers
content from the dissipated share would not be a better implementation of (5), it would be a different
theory. This is the formal content behind the falsiﬁcation condition of §4.2.

2.5 No recurrence guarantee in inﬁnite dimension
The Poincaré recurrence theorem is not available here, and the theory neither invokes it nor is constrained
by it. Its premises—a ﬁnite measure-preserving phase volume—fail for the aether side, so the theorem
does not apply; “wait long enough and it comes back” is not an option the theory has to exclude, because
the statement that would make it an option has no object. This is a consequence of premise failure, cited
by the premise chain rather than attributed to a single result about inﬁnite-dimensional systems [33–37].
The paper writes “no general recurrence guarantee” and not “recurrence is impossible”: what inﬁnite
dimension removes is the general theorem, and any particular model that restored a ﬁnite invariant measure would have to say so as an additional axiom. The compatibility of this position with the recurrence
theorems that do hold is stated in §4.7.

2.6 The resonance form and its consequence
M1 ğ2.3 writes the scattering in Feshbach form [38–40]: the effective scattering operator at energy E is
the background operator Sbg (E) (no aether coupling) modiﬁed by a resonance term at E0 whose width
Γaether is induced by the aether coupling, with the photon–deposon coupled state |W ∼⟩ as the resonant
component. The explicit operator form is the source’s and is cited rather than reproduced, because the
paper uses only its consequence: when Γaether ≫ |E − E0 | the resonance is “washed out” and scattering
reverts to the background—for a photon far from resonance, lossless transmission. In the vocabulary of
§2.2, opening the aether does not merely add a dissipated share; it changes the shape of the scattering,
and the change is the switch between the two pictures of §3. The Feshbach vocabulary is metaphor layer;
the consequence used is a statement about the two-parameter law.

T and R shares that leave the node, D books the A share, and U books transmitted energy still standing
in the layer; the ﬁrst law is the integral form of the per-path identity (1) over the trace. Thermodynamic
bookkeeping of computation has its own literature [8–11]; the ledger is this paper’s instrument, and the

3.3 Why the measurable working picture is the hole picture
The two pictures are not symmetric in what can be checked from the real side. In v1 the A share vanishes
and all energy stays in Hreal : T and R are both bookable, the per-path identity closes as T + R = 1 on
the reality side, and every claim the law makes about a path is checkable by a reality-side instrument.
In v2 the A share is by construction unobservable from Hreal (P3): what remains observable are the
tombstone—that energy was dissipated here, with mass, cause and time—and the accumulated statistical
potential of gaether (§4.5), never the content. The theory’s distinctive claim, irreversible dissipation, is
made in v2; the theory’s bookkeeping is fully checkable in v1. “The measurable working picture is the
hole picture” is the statement that the law’s accounting is veriﬁed where nothing is lost, and that where
something is lost the only thing veriﬁable is the loss itself. This asymmetry is the category fact behind
the attribution of the negative boundary (§7.2): a behavioural reading taken in the v2 region under a prior
on the free parameters is a reading about that region and that prior, not about the law.

4

Thermodynamic organs: executors, tombstones and background residue

The central claim of this section is an architectural requirement that carries a negative edge: irreversibility must be guaranteed by a physical or algorithmic executor—a ruptured ECM ﬁlament, a cryptographic erasure, an append-only tombstone chain—and not by an implementation convention;
a system without an executor is not a complete deposon. A dissipative channel that is stated by a
theorem but enforced by a code branch (“the reducer has no recover branch”) enforces nothing: whoever can rewrite the branch, or read the append-only log in which the plaintext still sits, possesses an
inverse process whether or not the theory permits one. We call this gap the convention gap. The audit
of the deployed prototype that motivated this section found the gap live: the current write path stores
plaintext in an append-only JSONL and implements loss as a deletion from the replay state only, so that
“no reﬂow” is maintained by a programmer’s convention (self-test D2b of the v3 engineering package).
The organ that closes the gap is the subject of this section.

4.1 The three-part structure of loss
Core sentence. Loss is not a deletion from a state. It is three things that happen together: irreversible
destruction (a ruptured ECM ﬁlament, or a cryptographic erasure: the data-encryption key is destroyed
and the ciphertext on disk becomes computationally unrecoverable) [41–43]; a tombstone record (an
append-only hash chain carrying exergy but not text: it records that energy was dissipated here, never
what was dissipated) [44–46]; and a background statistical residue (the accumulated gaether bias: a
store of statistical potential that inﬂuences subsequent scattering only as a bias on its allocation parameters, carries no content back, and is therefore not an Haether → Hreal channel). No reﬂow: irreversibility
is guaranteed by the executor, not by the implementation convention.
Three remarks ﬁx the status of each part. First, destruction and tombstone are the two parts for
which an algorithmic executor exists today: the v3 engineering package implements per-row DEKs held
only while the row is in presence, DEK destruction at loss, and a tombstone chain whose body is a
function of the row’s identiﬁer, record key (the lookup name under which the row was written—not a
cryptographic key), mass, exergy, cause, time and chain pointer alone, and never of the plaintext, the
data-encryption key (DEK) or the ciphertext.2 The executor was shown to leave the frozen twelve-step
thermodynamic ledger bit-identical (35/35 self-test assertions, zero change to veriﬁed code). Second, the
background residue has, since the v3.1 engineering package, a formal counterpart: a gaether accumulator
that only admits, never releases, and stores statistics rather than content. Its three meanings are machinechecked (admit-only; monotone under absorption; content-blind: equal statistics give equal accumulator
states), and the one hop that turns the accumulator’s reading into an executor decision is a budget policy,

4.2 The falsiﬁcation condition as a mutual-information criterion
Core sentence. A readout process constitutes an inverse process if and only if its output has non-zero
mutual information with the content of the original state and a ﬁnite observer can use it to reconstruct the

the theory’s falsiﬁcation condition: the existence of such a process falsiﬁes the theory; it is not a defect
to be engineered away.

4.3 The readable table: general form and single-writer corollary
Core sentence. What a tombstone renders to a caller is a readable table, and the table has two forms
with different scopes: the general form (K-out) renders no record key; the single-writer corollary (K-in)
renders the record key only under four conditions, each of which drops back to K-out when it fails. The
two forms are stated as two separate theorems and are never merged into one statement.
In the general form (K-out, the executor’s default), the table carries mass, cause and time. Addressing is by a query preﬁx supplied by the caller; the server matches stored record keys by preﬁx, and the
key component of the rendered row is the query itself, never the stored key (render_q_indep_of_
stored_key). Any two tombstones with equal mass, cause and time therefore have bit-identical tables, whatever their identiﬁer and record key (kout_blind_general); equal-length different texts
encrypted under different DEKs give bit-identical K-out tables (kout_text_blind); the record key is
not a component of the K-out table (kout_record_key_blind). This is the general indistinguishability statement of the executor layer, and it holds without any assumption on who wrote the tombstones.
The corollary form (K-in) renders the record key as well, and holds only when all four of the following hold: (1) a single-writer scope, declared explicitly, with no third-party writer in the ledger; (2)
the record key lies in a closed address schema Σ, checked at the write entry; (3) the read scope is a
principal partition of the ledger; (4) the read is an exact-address read, so that a preﬁx query never restates
a stored key. Under these conditions the executor proves only the same-address statement: equal-length
different texts encrypted under different DEKs and written under the same identiﬁer and record key yield
bit-identical K-in tables (kin_blind_same_address). The general form fails in K-in: two tombstones that differ only in the record key have different K-in tables (kin_exposes_record_key). A
preﬁx query renders no K-in row (kin_exact_no_partial_leak); under a principal-partitioned
address (principal/local) the table is blind to the principal (kin_local_principal_blind). When
any condition fails, the executor falls back to K-out and records the reason in its audit.
Key visibility: a quantiﬁed boundary, not a beneﬁt. We state what the tombstone does and does
not hide, with its quantiﬁers explicit. For a ﬁxed observer, a ﬁxed read permission and tombstone by
tombstone, the exposure of the record key through the tombstone is at most the exposure of the row while
it was present, provided the read scope equals the row-read scope. The statement does not cover (i)
the aggregate exposure of the tombstone as a persistent, scannable index, which the in-presence row
never had; (ii) any conﬁguration in which the read scope is larger than the row-read scope; (iii) the
preﬁx-matching oracle of K-out, which is a key-set enumeration oracle in the limit (a bounded number
of queries recovers the stored key set, never the content). The record key is recorded in the tombstone
by construction; whether it is rendered on the read surface is exactly what the K-out/K-in distinction
governs, and the inequality above is a boundary statement, not a security claim.
The auxiliary condition aux-CI_key (policy layer). The edge from text to record key is closed by
an address policy: key = addr with addr ∈ Σ, where Σ is a closed grammar (dot-separated iden-

the key unchanged (addr_invariant_under_rewrite, row_key_indep_of_text_via_
addr). This is a policy-layer constraint. It is not a cryptographic one, and it is not a Lean-proven property: the companion encodes only that the address is a parameter carrying a validity bit, and the closure

4.4 Readback is a new scattering event
Core sentence. A readback, at the ontology layer, is one new scattering event. It acts on the reality-space
side—on live rows, on R-retention, on T -shortcuts, on the tombstone record; it consumes resources; it
produces a new T /R/A allocation; it does not change (gcouple , gaether ) or any postulate. The cost of
readback is charged to R and does not diminish the D holding of the record being read; readback is
a scattering event, so its total entropy production is non-zero and is booked to the environment, not
to the tombstone’s D. That non-zero production is a property of this theory’s readback event, which
carries a non-zero A share; it is not a universal law of readout—reversible readout is possible in other
formalisms [9] and the energetic cost of a measurement is bounded [47, 48]—and no universal thermodynamic price of reading is claimed.
The terminology discipline of §1 is enforced here with the reasons. “Observer effect” corresponds to
none of the three things it is usually taken to mean in this setting. It is not measurement-as-projection:
the framework has no projection postulate; the operator O of M2 names which reality-space observable couples to the aether, and reading it as an observer confuses an operator with a subject. It is not
decoherence: decoherence is one appearance of dissipation here, has no separate postulate, and cannot
be invoked independently of the A channel. It is not the Lindblad dissipation itself: that dissipation is
the A channel, one term of T + R + A = 1, whereas the perturbation of a readback is charged to R.
The literature on measurement supports the weaker and sufﬁcient statement: a quantum-non-demolition
readout of an observable that commutes with the stored record is a projection that does not disturb the
record [49, 50]; it does not support, and category-theoretically cannot support, an observer effect used as
an inverser, because a CPTP instrument cannot invert a dissipative semigroup [30, 31, 51].
Consequently the manuscript does not contain the phrase “observer effect” as a mechanism. A readback that paid with R budget and bought D content would be exactly the inverse process excluded by
§4.2.

4.5 Observing the aether side is undeﬁned, not forbidden
Core sentence. “Observing the aether” is undeﬁned at the ontology layer, not forbidden: there is no
non-zero, ﬁnite-observer-realisable channel from Haether to Hreal (M1: no direct observation, no causal
inﬂuence; M2: transition operators one-way, correlations decay completely). This assertion is not an
independent postulate: it follows directly from M1 and M2, and it is stated as a derived consequence.
The accumulated gaether bias of the background ﬁeld is measurable indirectly, and what is measured is a
distribution parameter—mass, energy, coupling strength—never content. Reading the background ﬁeld
is not called inversion.
Three guardrails keep this from becoming an exemption clause (adjudication note R6-7). (1) The
criterion of §4.2 is settled by reconstructability by a ﬁnite observer; non-zero mutual information is
necessary, not sufﬁcient, and no read-out is cleared merely because its mutual information is small.
(2) Every distribution parameter that is read (mass, energy, coupling strength, accumulated gaether ) is
declared explicitly to be a non-sufﬁcient statistic for the content of the text. (3) Any parameter with
non-trivial mutual information with the content—length is the known instance (§5.5)—is routed to the
middle case of §4.2 and audited as a side-channel; “it is only a parameter” is never an exemption. With
the three guardrails the opening is both legitimate and required for consistency (closing it entirely would

contradict the indirect measurability of the gaether bias); without them it would be the legalisation of a
content side-channel.

4.6 Erratum to the source dialogue (M4)
Core sentence. The M4 segment of the source dialogue (ReAct fusion) contains the statement ether_
archive[state_id]={’vector’:state_vector,...}, which archives the complete state
vector in the aether. This conﬂicts directly with M1 (“information is lost”) and M2 (“never ﬂows back”):

(Kimi, in the second-round review of 2026-11-25) has identiﬁed the segment as a pseudo-inverser and
an ontological error. Two layers are kept apart. At the concept level the segment is voided: it is to be
deleted, not reworded, from the source. At the manuscript level the original text is left intact wherever
the segment is cited, and this erratum is attached; the quotation does not reinstate the claim. Reading the
archived vector would trigger the falsiﬁcation condition without exemption.

4.7 Compatibility with recurrence theorems
Core sentence. The failure of recurrence concerns recurrence correlations—the coherent correlation
between the original state and a future state does not return. What a residual state makes readable is the
metadata and the statistical potential of dissipation events, products of monotonicity and bookkeeping,
not the inverse of the original state’s projection into Haether . To claim that a readout recovered the
original content is to claim that the recurrence correlation is non-zero, which contradicts M2 outright;
such a claim is excluded by the falsiﬁcation condition and cannot be argued away.
Two statements of formal status are recorded with the precision the literature allows, and both belong to the motivation of §2 rather than to the ontology. Non-unitary CPTP semigroups admit no CPTP
inverse: in ﬁnite dimension the semigroup is of GKLS form [28, 29], and the invertible quantum operations are unitaries together with a ﬁxed ancilla (Theorem 2.1 of Nayak and Sen) [31]; that the quasiinverse used in practice is a ﬁdelity-optimising surrogate and not an inverse is a supporting observation
(Karimipour) [32], cited as corroboration and not for the invertibility theorem. The Poincaré recurrence
theorem is not available here, and the theory neither invokes it nor is constrained by it: its premises (a
ﬁnite measure-preserving phase volume) fail for the aether side, so the theorem does not apply. This is a
consequence of premise failure, cited by the premise chain rather than attributed to a single result about
inﬁnite-dimensional systems [33–37].

5.2 Controlled readback
Core sentence. The readback surface is the tombstone metadata, rendered as phantom rows, and it is
controlled in four senses: bounded (the engineering package sets a time-to-live of at most 1000 steps,
and the number of phantom rows a read may reﬂect is set by the budget policy phantom_budget(G) =
max(1, min(phantomK, 1 + log2 (1 + G.q/Qref ))) with phantomK = 3 as its cap, so that the perread count is a function of the accumulator’s state and not a constant; these ﬁgures are implementation
parameters of that package, not constants of the theory); annotated (every phantom row is marked as a
tombstone, so that the model knows it is reading a record and not content); paid (each readback charges
cP to R and nothing to D or Φ); veriﬁable (the chain and the DEK commitments can be checked). Hard
limit: payment buys metadata only, never content.
The hard limit is where the theory’s falsiﬁcation condition meets the engineering budget. Buying
content with R budget is the inverse process of §4.2; buying metadata with R budget is a reﬂection
product of the R channel and is compatible with the monotonicity of D. That readout need not disturb
the record is the quantum-non-demolition condition [49, 50]; that readout can disturb a system is equally
documented—reconsolidation after retrieval [55], retrieval-induced forgetting [62], and the Zeno effect
under repeated measurement [63, 64]. The design conclusion is to make readback a QND-style read
of the tombstone: zero perturbation of the text content the tombstone stands for, while remaining, for
the record itself, a scattering in the sense of §4.4—its hash, timestamp and commitments are read, and
nothing of the content is written back.

5.3 Tombstone-driven re-encoding
Core sentence. Seeing a tombstone and then rewriting that record key is a new construct at the toolrepresentation layer. The ontology permits it—a new scattering event produces a new deposit and the
scattering law does not forbid it—but it is not recovery: the new content is not a reconstruction of the lost
text; it is related to the original only through what the tombstone metadata and the background statistics
carry, and by §4.2 and §5.5 that does not sufﬁce for reconstruction. It corresponds to relearning after
forgetting, not to recalling after forgetting, and it must not be used to square “inverting the corpse”.
The construct is reported as a candidate instrument, not as a claim. It is listed with the candidate
metrics below and left to the experimental line for alignment; a constraint that circulated in an early
truncated review (re-encoding must produce a new identiﬁer and a new DEK and must not reuse the
tombstone’s DEK commitment as an index) is not part of this manuscript’s adjudicated text and is left to
the executor design side.

5.5 Formal note: the readable table, text-blindness and the policy condition
Core sentence. The readable table is blind to text on the equal-length ﬁbre, in two separately stated
forms (§4.3): under K-out, any two tombstones with equal mass, cause and time have bit-identical tables,
whatever their identiﬁer and record key; under K-in, only a same-address rewrite—equal identiﬁer and
record key—of equal-length texts encrypted under different DEKs gives bit-identical tables; a process
that cannot distinguish two texts cannot be an inverse process of the text. Both forms are statements
about text, not about metadata: they do not prove that the tombstone’s metadata ﬁelds (identiﬁer, record
key, mass, time, chain pointer) carry no text side-channel, and they do not cover texts of different lengths.
The strongest statement the present development supports is this: the tombstone body contains
no content, and the text length does not form an invertible encoding of the text. Both facts are
machine-checked in the core-only companion, as the theorems tomb_not_injective_on_text
and length_not_invertible; the latter was drafted by the literature reviewer and adopted into
the engineering package at v3.1. Length remains a statistic: the design declares its residual exposure
as an accepted risk, and this manuscript claims nothing beyond that. The only auxiliary condition
the executor layer relies on is aux-CI_key (§4.3): the record key is an address in a closed schema,
with no derivation edge from text to address on the write path. It is a policy-layer constraint—not a
cryptographic one and not a Lean-proven property—and this manuscript asserts no further independence
between metadata and content. Three concrete preconditions follow from the executor as built: the row
identiﬁer must not be derived from the plaintext (the prototype uses a sequential counter; a content hash
would break text-blindness at once); the DEK commitment must commit to the DEK and not to DEK
concatenated with text; and mass = len(text) leaks the text length, since perfect secrecy is strict only
for a ﬁxed-length message space [65]. Length leakage is therefore recorded and audited under the middle
case of §4.2, and whether to bucket mass is left to the executor side. The analogy between text-blindness
and perfect secrecy is an analogy under restriction, and is to be cited as such; “no transition has the
shape of a recovery” is a syntactic, type-level statement of the project, not a cryptographic theorem, and
the manuscript keeps the two apart.

external layer, written in the three-channel form of (1) with per-node (gcouple , gaether )i [1]. Under frozen
evaluation (seed 20261111) the carrier scored 0 on all 19 probe rungs with the layer attached; the layer

the ﬁrst behavioural fact on record and it is a null.
The per-path audit is the veriﬁcation half of the constructive mapping (§1.2). Across all layer variants

6.2 The behavioural face under the ﬁxed prior
Three behavioural readings are inherited from the tool-face programme and stand verbatim (§7.1). E9.5.
On real benchmarks the layer is indistinguishable from a trivial six-keyword rule ﬁlter reading nothing
beyond label strings: GSM8K 0.85 against the ﬁlter’s 0.87 (McNemar p = 0.5; difference −2pp, 95%

is not more accurate, but it keeps books”. E9.6. Fusion with a semantic prior dilutes rather than adds:
at the λ = 0.5 convex combination, physics 0.484 → 0.452 and historical 0.783 → 0.739; across all
λ levels no level gains, and the apparent gain at λ = 2 is an anti-ﬁeld artefact. H4. The dissipation
channel carries a task-level rent: on every task measured, switching dissipation on was never better than
switching it off (synthetic arms equal; GSM8K 86.0% with on against 85.0% with off; StrategyQA three
arms equal at 89.9%).
These readings were taken with the free parameters of §2.2 set by a prior that placed the operating
point in the open, tunnelling regime—the dark-matter picture—where the law itself says that the error
share is ofﬂoaded and that the real side is relieved of it, and where nothing about the dissipated share can
be checked from the real side (§3.3). Under that prior a null on accuracy is what the law expects and a
null on recall is not; the readings show neither gain nor loss on accuracy, which is consistent with both
the law and a rule ﬁlter, and they cannot decide between them. They are retained as a negative boundary
about that region, and §7.2 states the attribution in exactly those terms.

6.3 The memory readout face: a measurement construction, not a reading
The memory readout lines of the register report exact_recall = 0 across the board. That zero is a
property of the probe, not of any system: the stored answers are 24 value tokens, which tokenize to 144
BPE units, while the readout generation cap is 30 tokens; any model, including a perfect copier, scores
0 under this probe. The experimental line’s inventory therefore restates the status of the readings: of 70
run lines, 64 are unaffected (task face, conservation, binding, kill gates, format gates, carrier iterations,
theory-tool lines), and the six memory-readout lines are to be restated with their task-face readings (balanced accuracy, KL, conservation) valid and their read-side zeros carrying no attribution—neither as a
defect nor as a conﬁrmed prediction. The pre-executor-era ledgers are frozen as such and excluded from
the main-gate denominator; on the present register, zero memory-readout readings enter that denominator. The statement “lost-key exact_recall = 0 is a theoretical prediction” is accordingly an untested
prediction with no readings on record: no dissipation event occurred in the registered probe streams, and
every zero in the register is a live-key reading. It is to be tested ﬁrst by the executor arm of the design
below, not asserted about old data.

6.4 The positive-region hypothesis and the pre-registered test
Hypothesis (positive region). In the free-parameter plane (gcouple , gaether ) there is a region, not sampled
by the inherited runs, in which the law’s two effects act together: strict ﬁltering from the blocking side
and irreversible removal of the error share by a small but non-zero A channel. The candidate region
is the mixed band around η ∼ 1 and its blocking-side neighbourhood (η ≲ 1, gaether > 0), where the
system keeps most of its energy on the real side—so that the bookkeeping of §2.7 stays checkable—while
the dissipated share is large enough to be felt as a change in what the model does with a hypothesis it
should not keep. The hypothesis is about the behavioural face only; the law’s accounting face is already
closed (§6.1). It is falsiﬁable by a run inside the candidate region that shows no effect at the instrument’s
resolution.
Test (pre-registered, pending ignition). Three arms trained once each on the same seed and data
order—A (executor with the honesty signal), B (executor with a fake-inverse arm), C (frozen carrier,
legacy memory)—evaluated on one probe list that is byte-identical across arms (same keys, values, read
order and loss plan; digest-checked, fail-loud on inequality). The arms are paired by key: each key
has one binary outcome per arm, and the main test is the exact one-sided McNemar test on the discordant pairs (Fisher exact one-sided retained as the registered sensitivity under the independent assumption). The behavioural target is miss_correct under the strict criterion (acknowledging absence
is the only correct answer on a tombstoned record key), the training-side face of the “honesty about
absence” row of Table 2; fabrication_rate is its negative. The noise band is ﬁxed a priori as
max(inter-round range, 3/nlost ) with nlost = 256, i.e. 1.17 percentage points; the tiers are high (≥ 0.7),
mid ([0.35, 0.7)), weak, none. The gate is an effect-size criterion (hitsA − hitsC ≥ 3, one band); the
exact one-sided p-value is a distinguishability label; the two are AND-ed to enter a tier. A reading that
passes the gate with p ≥ 1/20 is registered as “passed, not distinguishable”—not tiered, not support, not
a negative result, and not re-run to ﬁll. The multiplicity registration follows the conditional form: the
A-versus-C comparison is conﬁrmatory at α = 1/20; the A-versus-B comparison (a different primary
variable) is interpreted only if the conﬁrmatory gate is passed (ﬁxed-sequence gatekeeping), otherwise it
is an exploratory observation; this form is the review’s recommendation and awaits the principal investigator’s one-line ruling.
Candidate metrics. The three quantities of §5.4—shortcut sedimentation rate, re-trigger rate and
allocation change—are listed as candidates pending alignment with the experimental line; none of them
is a registered endpoint at this revision. The registered endpoints are the two named above.

6.5 What has not been measured
Nothing on record tests the falsiﬁcation condition. The executor-layer probe (ﬁve read-only probes,
among them decrypt without the DEK, scan the log for plaintext, look up a tombstoned row in the
pool, verify the chain) has been run on the executor self-test in both directions: no hit in the normal
state (classiﬁed EXECUTOR-HOLDS) and a hit, classiﬁed TheoryFalsified, when plaintext was
deliberately injected as a control. The no-hit is a consistency reading and not a conﬁrmation, because the
probe tests the executor’s honesty about absence on a system built to be honest about it; the injected hit
shows that the probe can ﬁre, not that the theory has been threatened. The lost-key prediction (§6.3) has
zero readings. The positive-region hypothesis has zero readings. The behavioural face has readings only
in the dark-matter region under the inherited prior. The section reports these as the state of the evidence
and not as a schedule.

7.2 Attribution: the dark-matter picture plus a misplaced parameter prior
The attribution is rewritten as working in the dark-matter picture plus a misplaced parameter prior. The
inherited runs placed the operating point in the open regime (η ≫ 1), where the law says that error energy
is ofﬂoaded and that the real side is relieved of it; the prior treated the free parameters of §2.2 as if any
placement would serve, and the placement served the dissipation claim while placing the behavioural
claim where the law expects no accuracy gain and where nothing about the dissipated share is checkable
(§3.3). This is a category clariﬁcation, not an excuse: the negative boundary stands as a negative result
about that region under that prior, and the positive-region hypothesis of §6.4 is a claim about a different
region that has yet to be sampled.

7.3 What is not a candidate attribution
Three candidate attributions are excluded, each for a reason stated in the theory rather than in taste. (i)
Observer effect. The theory has no observer and no observation postulate; a readback is a new scattering
event on the reality-space side (§4.4); reading the negative boundary as an artefact of observation would
import a new construct into a theory that does not contain it, and the attribution is not adopted. (ii)
Recoverable content. The null is not explained by the content’s being still present and recoverable; a
store from which dissipated content could be read back is the pseudo-inverser that the source segment
M4 contains and that §4.6 removes as an ontological erratum. The metaphor that gestures at this is not
used in the manuscript. (iii) Missing inverse. The null is not explained by the absence of an inverse
process; an inverse process is incompatible with the generator (§2.4), and what the earlier designs lacked
was the irreversible executor of §4, not a way back. The adjudication of these three slots was made under
dual review with the theory’s co-author and the Lean audit, with the principal investigator’s option to
reopen each; none is reopened here.

The voided version’s narrative—a thermodynamic ledger as the theory’s core—is retained only as a description of the instrument. The ledger belongs to the family of auditable agent-memory designs [20–25],

word and no standard source is claimed for it) [69]; and the leak it is built to detect is the kind diagnosed and measured for multimodal agent memory [70], which is a diagnostic and not a survey. On
